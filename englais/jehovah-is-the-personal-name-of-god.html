Jehovah Is the Personal Name of God

Why the Bible presents God with a personal name · Bible Word God

God Has a Name

It is common to speak of God only with titles: the Creator, the Almighty, the Lord. These are true and respectful titles, but the Bible goes further. It presents God with a personal name, the very one he chose in order to make himself known. In English, this name is written Jehovah. God himself declares it in Isaiah: “I am the LORD: that is my name” (Isaiah 42:8).

This sentence is very clear. It does not say “it is one of my titles,” but “that is my name.” It is God’s way of telling us who he is, with the same simplicity as a person who says “my name is such and such.”

Texts That Confirm It

Psalm 83:18 affirms that God’s name is Jehovah and that he is the Most High over all the earth (Psalm 83:18). In Exodus 3:15, God tells Moses that this is his name forever and his remembrance from generation to generation (Exodus 3:15). And in Joel, we read the promise that whoever calls on the name of Jehovah will be saved (Joel 2:32).

Notice that the name is not a relic of the past. God says it is “forever.” What was true for Moses is true for us today.

Title and Name: A Useful Difference

“God” is a title that describes what he is: the supreme being, worthy of worship. “Lord” describes his authority. But “Jehovah” is his personal name, just as “Peter” or “Mary” are the names of persons. A title can apply to several; a name identifies only one. The Bible uses the word “god” even for false idols, but there is only one Jehovah.

That is why the Psalms speak of Jehovah when they want to stress the personal relationship: “The LORD is my shepherd” (Psalm 23:1). David does not say “God is a shepherd” in a general way, but addresses the God he knows and who knows him.

A Name That Invites Closeness

Whoever gives their name opens the door to friendship. When God revealed his name to Moses, he did so in the context of a promise: he was going to deliver his people. He said that he had seen Israel’s affliction, heard their cry and would come down to rescue them. His name was tied to his compassion.

In Jesus Christ we see this closeness brought to its fullest. Jesus taught us to pray by saying “Our Father” and said that he had made the Father’s name known (John 17:26). Knowing God’s personal name and calling him Father are not opposed; they complement each other. The name reveals who he is; fatherhood reveals how he loves us.

Respect and Reverence

Knowing God’s name is not a license to treat it lightly. The third commandment reminds us that we must not take his name in vain (Exodus 20:7). To use it with reverence means to honor it by our words and by our lives. A believer honors God’s name when he prays sincerely, when he keeps his promises and when he lives in a way that reflects the character of his Lord.

Some, out of fear of making a mistake, avoid pronouncing the name. But the Bible encourages us to call on it, to sing it and to make it known. What matters is not perfect pronunciation, but a humble heart.

An Answer for Today

If God has a name and wanted us to know it, then we can speak to him with familiarity and respect. You can say aloud: “Jehovah, thank you for making yourself known.” You will see how prayer stops being a ritual and becomes a dialogue.

We invite the reader to open their Bible to the indicated texts, to meditate on each sentence and to ask the Lord to teach them to know him better. Whoever has a name wants to be called by that name, and he promises to answer those who seek him.

Something to Meditate On

Think of the people closest to you. You probably call them by name and they call you by yours. In the same way, God knows you by your name. The prophet Isaiah wrote that God calls his people by name and does not forget them. May this truth go with you today: the God who has a name also knows yours, and speaks it with love.
