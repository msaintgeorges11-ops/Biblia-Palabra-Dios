📖 Book of Ephesians

Saved by Grace, United in Christ · 6 chapters

Context

Paul wrote this letter from prison, probably in Rome, around the year 60. Ephesus was a large commercial city devoted to the worship of the goddess Artemis, and Paul had spent nearly three years there. Unlike other letters, Ephesians does not respond to a specific problem, but offers an overall vision of God's eternal purpose for the Church. It divides naturally into two parts: chapters 1 to 3 teach the doctrine, and chapters 4 to 6 explain how to live it.

Central Message

The central message is that God has saved us by grace, has united us in Christ and has called us to live in a manner worthy of that calling. We were dead in our trespasses and sins, but God, rich in mercy, made us alive with Christ. We are no longer strangers, but members of God's household, built up as a holy temple. From this new identity springs new conduct: unity, truth, purity, love in the home and spiritual firmness.

Structure of the Book

  • Chapter 1: the spiritual blessings in Christ, election and the prayer for wisdom and revelation.
  • Chapter 2: from death to life by grace, and the reconciliation of Jews and Gentiles in one body.
  • Chapter 3: the mystery revealed, Paul's ministry and his prayer for the love of Christ.
  • Chapters 4 to 5: the unity of the Church, putting off the old self and walking in love and in light.
  • Chapter 6: relationships in the home, work and the armor of God.

Main Themes

The first theme is saving grace. Paul explains that we are saved by grace, through faith, and that this is not from ourselves, but is the gift of God. No one can boast, but no one is left without purpose either, for we were created in Christ for good works that God prepared beforehand. Salvation begins in grace and leads to service.

The second theme is the unity of the Church. Christ tore down the wall that separated Jews and Gentiles and created in himself one new man. That is why Paul urges believers to keep the unity of the Spirit in the bond of peace. There is one body, one Spirit, one hope, one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all.

The third theme is practical life in the home and in spiritual warfare. Paul teaches husbands, wives, children, fathers, servants and masters to maintain relationships of respect and love, as those who serve Christ. Then he reminds us that the struggle is not against flesh and blood, and calls us to put on the armor of God: truth, righteousness, peace, faith, salvation, the Word and prayer.

Key Verses

  • Ephesians 2:8-10
  • Ephesians 4:1-3
  • Ephesians 4:32
  • Ephesians 6:10-11

Reflecting on the Verses

In Ephesians 2:8-10, Paul sums up the Gospel: saved by grace, and not by works, to live in the good works that God prepared. In Ephesians 4:1-3, he urges us to walk with humility, gentleness and patience, bearing with one another in love. In Ephesians 4:32, he reminds us that we must be kind and forgive one another, as God forgave us in Christ. And in Ephesians 6:10-11, the apostle calls us to be strong in the Lord and to put on his full armor.

Application for Today

Ephesians reminds us who we are before telling us what we must do: beloved children, redeemed and called. From this identity, we can watch over our words, forgive generously and build homes where the love of Christ is breathed. It also encourages us to take prayer and the Word seriously, which are the weapons with which we resist the schemes of the enemy.

Questions to Reflect On

  • Do I live from my identity in Christ or from my failures and fears?
  • What words or attitudes do I need to give up in order to build up others?
  • Which piece of the armor of God do I need to use more consistently?

Prayer

Father, thank you for saving me by your grace and bringing me into your family. Help me to live with humility, to forgive as you have forgiven me and to stand firm in your armor. Amen.
