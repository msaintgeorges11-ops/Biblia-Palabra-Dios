Theme: Crying Out to Jesus

The Two Blind Men of Jericho

⏱ Approximate duration: 5 min

📖 Today’s Verses

Matthew 20:29-34
Psalm 116:1-4

🙏 Today’s Prayer

Lord Jesus, as this day comes to a close I come to you with a grateful heart. Thank you for sustaining me from morning until now, in the easy moments and the hard ones. Thank you for the food, for the roof over my head, and for the people you placed in my path today. I know I do not deserve so much love, but you give it to me abundantly, and so I praise you.

I thank you because you are a God who listens. You hear the cry of the one who suffers, you stop for the one whom no one notices, and you respond with mercy. Today I bring before you what I still do not understand, the needs that remain open, and the people I love who are going through trials. Open my eyes, Lord, to see your hand at work even where it seemed you were far away.

Forgive me if today I was carried away by anxiety or if I stayed silent when I should have cried out to you. Take from me fear and restlessness, and give me peaceful sleep. May I wake tomorrow eager to follow you, like one who has seen your goodness with his own eyes. I rest in your arms, trusting that you keep watch over me all night long. In Jesus’ name, amen.

📚 Today’s Story

Jesus was leaving Jericho on his way to Jerusalem, and a great crowd was following him. Two blind men were sitting by the roadside. They could not see, they had no way of pushing through the crowd, and perhaps many had forgotten them long ago. But when they heard that Jesus was passing by, they did the only thing within their reach: they raised their voices.

They cried out: “Lord, Son of David, have mercy on us.” The crowd rebuked them and told them to be quiet, as the noise of the world so often does to those who suffer. But instead of giving up, they cried out even louder. Their need was too great and the opportunity too precious to remain silent.

Then something beautiful happened: Jesus stopped. In the middle of the crowd, in the middle of a journey with an enormous destination ahead, the Lord stopped for two men whom almost no one paid attention to. He called them and asked: “What do you want me to do for you?” Although he knew what they needed, he wanted to hear it from their own lips. They answered simply: “Lord, let our eyes be opened.”

Moved with compassion, Jesus touched the eyes of those men, and immediately they received their sight. The account ends with a phrase as brief as it is precious: they followed him. Those who had been healed did not stay at the edge of the road, but walked with their Healer. The story teaches us that Jesus hears the cry of the needy, that he stops for each one of us, and that his mercy gives us back the light to follow him with all our heart.

✝️ Today’s Message

At the end of the day, this story invites us to ask how we pray. The two blind men did not compose an elaborate speech; they simply cried out for mercy. God does not expect perfect words from us, but a sincere heart that recognizes its need. The psalmist wrote that he loved the Lord because he had heard his voice and his plea, and that as long as he lived he would call on him.

Notice also that the blind men did not let the crowd silence their voices. Perhaps today someone told you it was not worth insisting, or your own thoughts advised you to keep quiet. But Jesus stops for the one who perseveres in calling out to him. Your prayer is not lost in the air; it reaches ears that listen tenderly, even when the answer is not yet visible.

Notice, too, Jesus’ question: What do you want me to do for you? The Lord invites you to tell him frankly what is in your heart. You do not need to hide your fears or dress up your requests. Tell him what weighs on you, what you hope for, and what you do not understand, and then trust that his answer will be good and will come at just the right time.

A practical step for tonight: before you sleep, spend a few minutes talking with Jesus quietly and sincerely. Tell him in one sentence what you need, as the blind men did, and give thanks for three specific things from this day. Then hand over the worry that weighs on you most and leave it in his hands. If he stopped for two men beside a road, he also stops for you.

Thank you, Lord, for this day lived under your care. Now hand your burdens over to Jesus, who hears every cry, and let his peace guard your heart through the night. Sleep calmly and rest without fear: the one who has kept you until now will not turn away from you.
