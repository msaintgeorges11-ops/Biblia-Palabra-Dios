🌿 What Does the Bible Say About Envy and How Can You Protect Yourself From It?

A heart set free from envy

An ancient evil

Envy is as old as humanity. It appears early in the Bible and causes suffering in the family, in the church and in society. Understanding what God’s Word says helps us recognize it in our own hearts and overcome it. It also teaches us how to act when we are the ones facing the envy of others.

What is envy?

Envy is a feeling of sadness or resentment toward what another person has or is. It is different from admiration, which rejoices in the other’s good. Proverbs says that a tranquil heart is life to the body, but envy is rottenness to the bones (Proverbs 14:30). Envy does not only harm relationships; it also makes the soul sick.

Biblical examples

Cain was jealous of his brother Abel because God had accepted Abel’s offering, and envy led him to murder (Genesis 4:4-8). Joseph’s brothers envied him because of their father’s affection and sold him as a slave (Genesis 37:4). Saul was filled with jealousy toward David when he heard the people’s songs (1 Samuel 18:7-9). In every case, envy began in the heart and ended in destructive actions.

There is also a shining example: Jonathan, son of King Saul, loved David and defended him, even though David was to reign in his place (1 Samuel 18:1-4). His attitude shows that envy can be overcome.

The works of the flesh

Paul counts envy among the works of the flesh (Galatians 5:19-21). James warns that where there is jealousy and a spirit of strife, there is disorder and every kind of evil practice (James 3:16). That is why the Bible calls us to take it seriously and not to justify it.

How to overcome envy

Acknowledge it before God. Praying sincerely is the first step. God is not scandalized by it; he forgives and heals (1 John 1:9).

Practice gratitude. When you look at what God has given you, there is less room for comparison.

Rejoice with others. Paul commands us to rejoice with those who rejoice (Romans 12:15).

Trust God’s plan. He has a path for each person. Comparisons are often unfair.

Contentment. Paul learned to be content in every situation (Philippians 4:11-12).

When someone envies you

Sometimes, without meaning to, we awaken envy in others. Joseph experienced this. Yet he did not repay evil with evil and trusted in God, who in the end raised him up (Genesis 50:20). If someone envies you, pray for that person, do not boast about what you have and do not respond with revenge. Jesus taught us to love our enemies and to pray for those who persecute us (Matthew 5:44). God is your defender and your protection.

The root: insecurity

Very often, envy is born from doubt about our own worth. But the Bible says that we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works (Ephesians 2:10). Each person has different gifts, and all are needed, like the members of a body (1 Corinthians 12:18-20). When you understand that God has given you a unique place, you no longer need to compete or compare yourself.

A simple exercise

When you feel envy toward someone, do three things: thank God for something concrete that you have, pray that this person will be blessed, and ask God what he wants to teach you. With practice, envy loses its strength and turns into love.

A prayer against envy

Lord, search my heart. If there is envy in me, take it away and fill my heart with gratitude. Teach me to rejoice with others and to trust in your plan. Protect me from any envy directed at me and help me to respond with love. In Jesus’ name, amen.
