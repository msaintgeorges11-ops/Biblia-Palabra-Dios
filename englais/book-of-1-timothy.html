📖 Book of 1 Timothy

A Guide for the Church · 6 chapters

Context

Paul wrote this letter to Timothy, his son in the faith, whom he had left in Ephesus to oversee the church. Timothy was young, shy by nature, and faced false teachers, useless controversies and challenges of organization. Paul, now older and experienced, writes to him to encourage him, give him practical instructions and remind him how to conduct himself in the household of God. It is one of the so-called pastoral letters, along with 2 Timothy and Titus.

Central Message

The central message is how the Church, the pillar and foundation of the truth, should live and organize itself. Paul speaks of sound doctrine, prayer for all people, the character that overseers and deacons must have, care for widows, the treatment of elders and the attitude toward wealth. He asks the young Timothy to be an example in speech, conduct, love, spirit, faith and purity, and to fight the good fight of the faith.

Structure of the Book

  • Chapter 1: the warning against false teachers, Paul's gratitude for the mercy received and the charge entrusted to Timothy.
  • Chapter 2: prayer for all people and instructions on order in worship.
  • Chapter 3: the qualities of overseers and deacons, and the mystery of godliness.
  • Chapters 4 to 5: the example of the young leader and the care of widows, elders and workers.
  • Chapter 6: false teachers, contentment, the danger of money and the final recommendation.

Main Themes

The first theme is sound doctrine. Paul warns against those who teach something else, who get lost in myths and endless genealogies and stir up arguments instead of building up. The aim of the command is love that comes from a pure heart, a good conscience and a sincere faith. The truth is not defended with pride, but with love and with a life consistent with what one teaches.

The second theme is the character of leadership. Paul describes those who aspire to oversee the Church: above reproach, sober-minded, self-controlled, hospitable, able to teach and well thought of. Having gifts is not enough, for character matters as much as competence. He also gives instructions for deacons, stressing dignity, sincerity and faithfulness in service.

The third theme is godliness accompanied by contentment. Paul teaches that godliness is a source of great gain when accompanied by contentment, for we brought nothing into the world and we can take nothing out of it. The love of money is a root of all kinds of evils, and those who crave it pierce themselves with many pangs. Instead, Timothy must pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, patience and gentleness.

Key Verses

  • 1 Timothy 2:1-4
  • 1 Timothy 4:12
  • 1 Timothy 6:6-8
  • 1 Timothy 6:12

Reflecting on the Verses

In 1 Timothy 2:1-4, Paul asks us to pray for all people, even for those in authority, because God desires all to be saved. In 1 Timothy 4:12, he encourages Timothy not to let anyone despise his youth, but to be an example for the believers. In 1 Timothy 6:6-8, he teaches us that the true gain is godliness with contentment. And in 1 Timothy 6:12, he urges us to fight the good fight of the faith and to take hold of eternal life.

Application for Today

This letter reminds us that witness is worth more than age or position. Everyone, young or old, can be an example by the way they speak, treat others and live out their faith. It also invites us to pray consistently for those who govern and to examine our relationship with money. Contentment is a form of freedom that the world cannot offer.

Questions to Reflect On

  • What kind of example am I through my words and conduct?
  • Do I pray regularly for those in authority and for those who are far from God?
  • What would help me live with more contentment?

Prayer

Lord, make me an example of faith and love. Keep me from the love of money, give me wisdom to discern the truth and strengthen my heart to fight the good fight of the faith. Amen.
