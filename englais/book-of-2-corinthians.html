📖 Book of 2 Corinthians

Comfort in Weakness · 13 chapters

Context

This letter was written by Paul from Macedonia, shortly after sending the first one. Between the two there was a painful visit and a severe letter, and Paul had waited anxiously for news from Corinth. When Titus informed him that many had repented, the apostle wrote with relief and gratitude. However, he also had to defend his ministry against certain teachers who criticized him and boasted of impressive credentials. That is why it is the most personal and most moving of his letters.

Central Message

The central message is that God comforts his people and shows his power in weakness. Paul describes a ministry full of afflictions, but sustained by grace and by the hope of the glory to come. We carry a treasure in jars of clay, so that this great power may be attributed to God and not to us. The letter also speaks of reconciliation with God, of generosity and of the authenticity with which one who serves the Lord must live.

Structure of the Book

  • Chapters 1 to 2: God's comfort in affliction, forgiveness and the change in Paul's plans.
  • Chapters 3 to 5: the ministry of the new covenant, the treasure in jars of clay and the ministry of reconciliation.
  • Chapters 6 to 7: the call to holiness and Paul's joy at the Church's repentance.
  • Chapters 8 to 9: the generous collection for believers in need in Jerusalem.
  • Chapters 10 to 13: the defense of his apostleship, the thorn in the flesh and the final exhortations.

Main Themes

The first theme is comfort. Paul begins by praising the God of all comfort, who comforts us in all our afflictions so that we may comfort others with the comfort we ourselves receive. Suffering is not useless: it prepares believers to walk with empathy alongside those who are going through trials. Paul does not hide his anguish, but he looks at it in the light of God's faithfulness.

The second theme is strength in weakness. Paul speaks of a thorn in his flesh that was given to him to keep him from becoming proud. He prayed for it to be taken away, but the Lord answered him that his grace was sufficient and that his power is made perfect in weakness. Since then, Paul boasts in his weaknesses, for when he is weak, then he is strong in Christ.

The third theme is generosity and integrity. The Macedonians, in the midst of their poverty, gave abundantly and joyfully. Paul encourages the Corinthians to give willingly, without compulsion, for God loves a cheerful giver. He also insists on behaving with transparency, so that no one may find in his ministry an occasion for stumbling.

Key Verses

  • 2 Corinthians 1:3-4
  • 2 Corinthians 4:7
  • 2 Corinthians 5:17
  • 2 Corinthians 12:9

Reflecting on the Verses

In 2 Corinthians 1:3-4, God appears as the Father of mercies who comforts us so that we may comfort others. In 2 Corinthians 4:7, Paul acknowledges that we are fragile like jars of clay, but that we carry an immense treasure. In 2 Corinthians 5:17, he affirms that if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation: the old has passed away, everything has become new. And in 2 Corinthians 12:9, the Lord's answer to Paul becomes a promise for every believer who feels weak.

Application for Today

This letter teaches us to look at our weaknesses with different eyes. We do not have to feign strength or hide our struggles, because God's grace works precisely where we acknowledge our need. It also encourages us to turn our wounds into ministry: one who has been comforted can walk alongside others. And it invites us to give joyfully, knowing that God is faithful to provide for our needs.

Questions to Reflect On

  • What weakness do I need to hand over to God so that his power may show in it?
  • Whom can I comfort with the comfort I have received myself?
  • Do I give joyfully or out of obligation?

Prayer

Father of mercies, thank you for comforting me in my trials. Make me a useful vessel in your hands, strengthen me in my weakness and teach me to give with a joyful heart. Amen.
