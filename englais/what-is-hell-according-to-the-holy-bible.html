📚 What Is Hell According to the Holy Bible

An honest answer based on the Holy Bible · Bible Word God

What does the Bible say?

Hell is a serious subject, and the Bible treats it with seriousness and with love. Jesus spoke of "everlasting fire, prepared for the devil and his angels" (Matthew 25:41). Hell was not created for human beings, but for God's enemy.

The Scriptures describe it as separation from God: "Who shall be punished with everlasting destruction from the presence of the Lord, and from the glory of his power" (2 Thessalonians 1:9). Revelation 20:14-15 calls it the lake of fire, the final destiny of everything that opposes God.

A warning of love

Jesus warned: "Fear not them which kill the body, but are not able to kill the soul: but rather fear him which is able to destroy both soul and body in hell" (Matthew 10:28). Such a warning is born not of hatred, but of the concern of a Savior who does not want anyone to be lost.

There is good news

God "will have all men to be saved" (1 Timothy 2:4). That is why he sent his Son: "that whosoever believeth in him should not perish, but have everlasting life" (John 3:16). The God who warns is the same one who opens the door of salvation.

The words the Bible uses

When we read the Bible in English, we meet the word "hell," but in the original languages there are several terms. In the Old Testament we find Sheol, the abode of the dead. In the New Testament we find Hades, Gehenna and Tartarus. Each word has its own meaning, and understanding them helps us avoid mixing up ideas. What is clear, however, is that when Jesus speaks of Gehenna, he refers to God's final judgment on sin and evil.

The image of fire and darkness comes up again and again. Jesus speaks of "outer darkness," where "there shall be weeping and gnashing of teeth" (Matthew 25:30). These images express something no human word can fully describe: how terrible it is to live far from God, who is the source of all light, all love and all life.

Why it exists

God is holy and just. A God who ignored evil, violence and injustice would not be good. Hell is the ultimate consequence of rejecting God and his mercy. God respects the freedom he has given us: he does not force anyone who does not want to be with him. But he does so with sorrow, because his desire is for all to return to him.

That is why the Bible always joins judgment with the offer of grace. Peter writes that God is patient and does not want anyone to be lost. Paul says that "the wages of sin is death; but the gift of God is eternal life through Jesus Christ our Lord" (Romans 6:23). The first half of the verse warns us; the second invites us.

How to respond

There are two mistakes in facing this subject. The first is to ignore it, as if it had never been mentioned. The second is to use it to frighten or condemn others. Jesus never used it to humiliate; he used it to call to repentance and to awaken those who were sleeping in their indifference.

The right response is humility and gratitude. If we are in Christ, it is by pure grace, and not because we are better. This gratitude moves us to pray for those who do not yet know God, to speak to them with respect and to live in a way that the love of Christ can be seen.

To reflect on today

Ask yourself calmly: where is my trust today? In my works, in my family, in my savings, or in Jesus? The Gospel does not say "try harder," but "come to me." Jesus said: "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest" (Matthew 11:28).

If you felt fear while reading this article, do not ignore it. It is an invitation to draw near to God, not to move away from him. Talk to him in prayer, read the Gospel of John and look for a church where you can ask questions without fear.
