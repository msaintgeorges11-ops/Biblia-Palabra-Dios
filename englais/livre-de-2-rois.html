📖 Book of 2 Kings

The fall of the kingdoms · 25 chapters

Background

Like First Kings, this book is anonymous, and Jewish tradition mentions Jeremiah. Its final composition falls during the exile, after 561 BC, since it ends with the release of King Jehoiachin. It covers about three hundred years, from Ahaziah and the ministry of Elisha to the destruction of Jerusalem, and explains to the exiles why they lost their land.

Central message

Second Kings explains that Israel fell to Assyria and Judah to Babylon, not for lack of God's power, but because they persisted in idolatry and rejected the prophets. Yet God acts with mercy: Elisha displays his power, Hezekiah and Josiah reform the people, and the book ends with a glimmer of hope for the house of David. God's Word is fulfilled, in judgment as well as in grace.

Structure of the book

  • Chapters 1 to 8: Elijah is taken up to heaven and Elisha continues his work with miracles, Naaman, and the siege of Samaria.
  • Chapters 9 to 17: Jehu's purge, Athaliah, the kings of the two kingdoms, and the fall of Samaria to Assyria.
  • Chapters 18 to 20: King Hezekiah trusts in the Lord, the defeat of Sennacherib, and the king's illness.
  • Chapters 21 to 23: the wickedness of Manasseh, the book of the law found in the temple, and Josiah's reforms.
  • Chapters 24 and 25: the last kings of Judah, the destruction of Jerusalem, the exile, and the release of Jehoiachin.

Main themes

The first theme is the power of the prophetic word. Elisha receives a double portion of Elijah's spirit, heals Naaman, multiplies a widow's oil, and raises the Shunammite's son. These miracles show that God cares for the needy and for foreigners. The prophets warn constantly, and the kings who listen to them prosper, while the others suffer the consequences.

The second theme is reform and faith. Hezekiah trusts in the Lord before Sennacherib's army, and God delivers Jerusalem. Josiah, upon hearing the book of the law, tears his clothes and undertakes a deep reform. Their stories show that a return to the Word and sincere prayer can transform a nation, even if they are not enough to stop accumulated judgment.

The third theme is judgment and hope. The exile is presented as the result of generations of disobedience, but the book does not end in despair. The release of Jehoiachin in Babylon suggests that God has not forgotten his promise to David. Christians see in this the preparation for the King who would come and restore his people forever.

Key verses

  • 2 Kings 5:15
  • 2 Kings 6:16
  • 2 Kings 19:19
  • 2 Kings 22:11

Reflecting on the verses

In 2 Kings 5:15, Naaman, healed, acknowledges that there is no other God in all the earth except in Israel. 2 Kings 6:16 comforts with this word: those who are with us are more than those who are with them. In 2 Kings 19:19, Hezekiah begs God to deliver them so that all may know that he alone is the Lord. And 2 Kings 22:11 shows Josiah shaken upon hearing the law.

Application for today

Second Kings urges us to take God's Word seriously, reading it with a heart ready to be corrected. It also encourages us not to fear when opposing forces seem greater, because God is with his people. And it reminds us that no institution, however solid it may seem, replaces a living faith and sincere obedience.

Questions for reflection

  • Am I ready to let God's Word correct me, like Josiah?
  • What situation makes me feel surrounded, and how can I remember that God is with me?
  • What warnings from the Lord have I ignored that I should listen to today?

Prayer

Lord, your word is always fulfilled. Give me a sensitive heart like Josiah's, calm my fears with your presence, and keep me from ignoring your warnings. Be my hope in difficult times. Amen.
