Why Do Some Bibles Say Jehovah and Others Lord?

God’s name in Bible translations · Bible Word God

A Difference That Raises Questions

Many people notice that their Bible says “Jehovah” while a loved one’s says “the LORD.” Are these errors? Different Bibles? In reality, it is a difference in translation that has a long history. Understanding it will help you read with more confidence, whatever version you use.

In the original Hebrew text, the same four-consonant name, YHWH, appears in all these places. What changes is how each translation decides to render it.

The Origin of the Jewish Custom

Over the centuries, many Jews came to feel such respect for God’s name that they stopped pronouncing it, so as not to use it in vain (Exodus 20:7). In public reading, when the text had YHWH, they said “Adonai,” which means “Lord.” Later, in some manuscripts, vowel signs were added to recall this substitution.

This practice influenced the ancient translations. The Greek version called the Septuagint, used by many Christians of the first century, used the word “Kyrios” (Lord) to represent the divine name. Later, the Latin Vulgate followed a similar line with “Dominus.”

Translations That Use “Jehovah”

At the time of the Reformation, translators sought to return to the Hebrew. In the English-speaking world, many Bibles rendered the divine name as “the LORD,” following the tradition of the King James Version (1611) and later revisions such as the ESV and the NIV. The American Standard Version (1901) and other translations, notably the New World Translation, write “Jehovah.” The King James Version itself uses “Jehovah” in a few places. That is why it is normal to find “Jehovah” on this site and in many churches and publications.

Anyone who reads a Bible that preserves the name sees God’s name in passages such as “Jehovah is my shepherd” (Psalm 23:1) or “Call to me and I will answer you,” spoken by Jehovah (Jeremiah 33:3).

Translations That Use “Lord” or “LORD”

Many modern versions, in English as in other languages, write “LORD” (in capital or small capital letters) to represent the name YHWH, and “Lord” for Adonai. They do so out of respect for Jewish and Christian tradition, and because they believe it makes public reading easier. The capitalization is a clue: it tells us that the divine name is there.

Some translators add an explanatory note so that the reader knows that in these places the Hebrew says YHWH. Other versions, such as the Jerusalem Bible, use “Yahweh.”

Which Is Best?

The question has no single answer. Each translation has valid reasons. Those that keep the name help us see that God has a personal name and that the relationship with him is personal. Those that use “Lord” follow a long liturgical custom and stress his authority. None of them changes the message of salvation.

The wisest course is to read with humility, to compare when necessary and to remember that the Holy Spirit enlightens whoever approaches the Word with a sincere heart.

The Name in the New Testament

In the New Testament, written in Greek, the authors use “Lord” for the Father as well as for Jesus. When they quote the Old Testament, as in Romans 10:13, where Joel is quoted, they apply to Jesus what the prophet said about Jehovah (Romans 10:13). It is a powerful sign of the faith of the first Christians in the divinity of Christ.

This shows that “Lord” is not a minor title: it is a confession of faith. “Jesus Christ is Lord” (Philippians 2:11).

What to Do in Practice

If your Bible says “Jehovah,” give thanks for the name. If it says “Lord” or “LORD,” remember that behind it there is a name and a history. In both cases, what matters is to read, to believe and to obey.

We encourage you to compare the same passage in two versions. You will see that the message is the same. The difference lies only in the way of naming the same God, whom we worship with respect and trust.

A Tip for Study

If you wish to go deeper, open several versions of the same psalm and observe how each one translates the divine name. Note the differences, but also the similarities: you will see that all proclaim a faithful, holy and near God. It is also very beneficial to read the footnotes in your Bible, where it is often explained how the name was translated. Studying with patience protects against confusion and strengthens confidence in Scripture.
