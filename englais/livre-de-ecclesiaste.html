📖 Book of Ecclesiastes

Living under the sun with God · 12 chapters

Background

The book presents itself as the words of the Preacher, son of David and king in Jerusalem, whom tradition identifies with Solomon, although some scholars propose another author or a later date. It is a wisdom text that reflects on the meaning of life. It speaks to those who wonder whether work, pleasure, and wealth can satisfy the soul.

Central message

The central message is that everything sought apart from God proves to be vanity, a passing breath that does not fill the heart. The Preacher examines wisdom, pleasure, work, money, and power, and discovers that none offers lasting gain. But life received as a gift from God does have meaning. His conclusion is to fear God and keep his commandments, for he will judge every deed.

Structure of the book

  • Chapters 1 and 2: the theme of vanity and the vain search in wisdom, pleasure, works, and riches.
  • Chapters 3 to 5: the times set by God, oppression, envy, and caution when approaching the house of God.
  • Chapters 6 to 8: human limits, the value of wisdom, and the paradoxes of justice in this life.
  • Chapters 9 to 11: the certainty of death, the invitation to live with joy and diligence, and the value of boldness.
  • Chapter 12: the call to remember the Creator in youth and the Preacher's final conclusion.

Main themes

The first theme is the vanity of life without God. The recurring word is breath or vapor: something that vanishes. Working, accumulating, or achieving great things without reference to the Lord leaves an emptiness. The Preacher honestly expresses the frustration of a fallen world, where death makes everyone equal, and prepares the reader to seek something greater than the goods of this world.

The second theme is time and God's sovereignty. There is a time to be born and a time to die, a time to weep and a time to laugh. God has made everything beautiful in its time and has set eternity in the human heart, but we cannot understand all his work. We must accept our limits, trust in his purpose, and learn to enjoy with gratitude what he gives us each day.

The third theme is joy as a gift from God. The Preacher encourages us to eat, to drink, and to enjoy the fruit of our work, recognizing that it comes from the hand of the Lord. This is not hedonism, for he asks us to remember the Creator and to live in reverence. Everything points to Christ, who gives eternal meaning to a life that would otherwise be fleeting.

Key verses

  • Ecclesiastes 1:2
  • Ecclesiastes 3:1
  • Ecclesiastes 3:11
  • Ecclesiastes 12:13

Reflecting on the verses

In 1:2, the Preacher declares that everything is vanity, and so opens his examination of life. In 3:1, he affirms that there is a time for everything under heaven. In 3:11, he explains that God has made everything beautiful in its time and has put eternity in our hearts. And in 12:13, he sums up the whole duty of humanity: fear God and keep his commandments. All of this leads to a life full of reverence.

Application for today

Ecclesiastes invites us to examine what we pursue. If our goals depend only on success, money, or pleasure, we will end up exhausted. We can enjoy food, family, and work as gifts, accept the seasons of life, and seek God first. Remembering him today, not tomorrow, is the best way to live. In this way our work takes on meaning, because we do it for the Lord and not only for ourselves.

Questions for reflection

  • What things am I pursuing that truly cannot satisfy my soul?
  • How can I receive with gratitude what God gives me today?
  • What does it mean for me to remember my Creator at this stage of my life?

Prayer

Eternal God, you have set eternity in my heart. Free me from chasing after what vanishes. Teach me to receive each day as a gift, to live with joy, and to fear you with my whole being. Amen.
