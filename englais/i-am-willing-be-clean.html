Theme: I Am Willing; Be Clean

Jesus Heals a Leper

⏱ Approximate duration: 5 min

📖 Today’s Verses

Mark 1:40-42
Psalm 40:1-3

🙏 Today’s Prayer

Merciful Father, as night falls I come to thank you for this day. Thank you for your patience with me, for your care in the great and the small, and for all the times you listened to me without my noticing. I thank you because your love does not depend on what I do, but on who you are: a good God who draws near to the needy.

Lord Jesus, just as a sick man knelt before you and said: if you are willing, you can make me clean, today I too come to you in humility. You did not turn away from him, but stretched out your hand and touched him. Touch my life where there are wounds others do not see, where there is shame, weariness, or guilt. Tell me, too, that you are willing to heal me and restore my peace.

Thank you for lifting me out of the pit of despair and setting my feet on a rock. Put a new song in my mouth, a song of praise to you. I hand over to you my burdens from today, my pending matters, and my fears. Bless those I love and those who suffer tonight in loneliness or illness. Give me restful sleep and wake me tomorrow with renewed joy. Thank you, Lord, for always listening to me. In Jesus’ name, amen.

📚 Today’s Story

The Gospel of Mark tells that one day a man with leprosy came to Jesus. In that time, leprosy was not only a painful disease; it also cut a person off from family and community, because the law required him to live in isolation and to call out so that no one would come near him. That man carried, besides the pain, the loneliness of being avoided by everyone.

But this man did something brave: he came to Jesus, knelt before him, and begged: “If you are willing, you can make me clean.” His words show a simple faith. He demanded nothing and did not doubt the Master’s power; he only placed his need before him and left the decision in his hands, acknowledging that Jesus could do it.

Mark says that Jesus, moved with compassion, stretched out his hand and touched him. That gesture was enormous. According to the custom of his time, no one touched a leper. Jesus did not heal him with his word alone, but first touched him, showing him that he was not alone. And he said: “I am willing; be clean.” The text adds that immediately the leprosy left the man and he was made clean.

This story reminds us that Jesus always draws near to the one who draws near to him sincerely. He is not ashamed of our wounds nor does he turn away from our weaknesses. His response, “I am willing,” is one of the most comforting words in the Gospels. Whoever comes to Christ with his need finds an outstretched hand, true compassion, and a healing that reaches the body, the heart, and the dignity.

✝️ Today’s Message

We all carry some burden we would prefer not to show. It may be an old guilt, an illness, a family conflict, or a sadness we cannot explain. Like the leper, sometimes we feel our condition separates us from others. But the Gospel shows us a Jesus who draws near, and that changes everything.

Notice that man’s prayer: “If you are willing, you can make me clean.” It is a humble and trusting prayer, which recognizes Jesus’ power and leaves the decision in his hands. You can pray this way tonight. You do not have to dress up your words or hide what you feel. It is enough to be sincere and say to him: “Lord, you can. Here I am.”

Here is a practical application for today. Think of whether there is someone who has been left on the margins: a sick neighbor, a distant relative, or a coworker who is alone. Jesus touched the one everyone avoided. Tomorrow you can imitate that gesture with a call, a visit, a warm message, or an unhurried conversation. A gesture of closeness can be the beginning of healing in another person’s heart.

The psalmist says: “I waited patiently for the Lord; he inclined to me and heard my cry.” God bends down to those who seek him. Tonight you do not have to resolve anything on your own. Hand over your wound, your weariness, and your worry. The one who said “I am willing” to the leper looks at you with the same compassion. Rest, and trust that he will keep working in you.

May the Lord stretch out his hand over you tonight and tell you that he is willing to give you peace. Hand over to him what hurt today and rest without fear. Tomorrow you will see his mercy renewed, like a dawn that never fails.
