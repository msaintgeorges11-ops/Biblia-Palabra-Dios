📖 Book of Jude

Contending for the Faith · 1 chapter

Context

Jude, brother of James and also brother of Jesus according to the flesh, wrote this brief letter. Originally, he wanted to write about our common salvation, but he felt compelled to urge his readers to contend for the faith that was once for all delivered to the saints. Ungodly people had crept into the churches, turning the grace of God into a license for immorality and denying our only Master and Lord. The letter is brief, intense and full of images drawn from the Old Testament.

Central Message

The central message is the need to persevere in the true faith in the face of false teachers. Jude warns with examples of judgment: Israel in the wilderness, the angels who abandoned their dwelling, Sodom and Gomorrah, Cain, Balaam and Korah. He describes the intruders as waterless clouds, fruitless trees and wandering stars. But he also encourages believers to build themselves up in their most holy faith, to pray in the Spirit, to keep themselves in the love of God and to have compassion on those who doubt.

Structure of the Book

  • Verses 1 to 4: the greeting, the change of purpose and the warning about the intruders.
  • Verses 5 to 7: the examples of judgment of Israel, the fallen angels and Sodom and Gomorrah.
  • Verses 8 to 16: the description of the false teachers through images and examples.
  • Verses 17 to 23: the call to remember what the apostles said, to build oneself up and to show compassion.
  • Verses 24 and 25: the final doxology to the One who is able to keep.

Main Themes

The first theme is the defense of the faith. Jude asks us to contend for the faith, but not with violence or pride, rather with firmness in the truth and with faithfulness. The Christian faith is not a changing human invention, but a teaching delivered once for all. Defending it involves knowing it, living it and passing it on with integrity, without watering it down or making it a pretext for doing whatever one wants.

The second theme is the seriousness of judgment. The examples Jude cites show that God takes rebellion, immorality and contempt for his authority seriously. Although we live under grace, this grace is not a license to sin. On the contrary, the one who has been forgiven must respond with reverence and obedience. Judgment is also a reminder that God is just and that no evil will go unanswered.

The third theme is active compassion. After warning, Jude encourages believers to build themselves up, to pray and to keep themselves in the love of God while waiting for the mercy of our Lord Jesus Christ. He asks them to have mercy on those who doubt and to save others by snatching them out of the fire. Firmness in the truth is not incompatible with tenderness toward those who struggle, as long as one preserves one's own purity.

Key Verses

  • Jude 3
  • Jude 20-21
  • Jude 22-23
  • Jude 24-25

Reflecting on the Verses

In Jude 3, we are encouraged to contend for the faith delivered once for all to the saints. In Jude 20-21, a recipe is given for persevering: building ourselves up in the faith, praying in the Spirit and keeping ourselves in the love of God. In Jude 22-23, we are called to have compassion on those who doubt and to rescue others with caution. And in Jude 24-25, the letter ends with a doxology to the One who is able to keep us from stumbling and to present us with joy.

Application for Today

Jude invites us to take care of our spiritual life with intention. We can build ourselves up by reading the Bible, by daily prayer and by fellowship with other believers. He also asks us to keep our eyes open to teachings that present grace as a pretext for sinning. And he reminds us that we are not alone in perseverance: it is God who keeps us, and that is why we end with praise.

Questions to Reflect On

  • What habits help me to build myself up in the faith?
  • Is there someone who doubts whom I can accompany with patience?
  • How can I rest on God's power to keep me?

Prayer

To the only God, our Savior, be glory and majesty. Keep me from all stumbling, build me up in your truth and give me compassion for those who doubt. Amen.
