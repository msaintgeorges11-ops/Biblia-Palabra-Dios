Tonight

Theme: The One Thing Needed

Martha and Mary at Bethany

⏱ Approximate duration: 5 min

📖 Today’s Verses

Psalm 134:1-3
Luke 10:41-42

🙏 Today’s Prayer

Heavenly Father, as I end this day, I come to you with a grateful heart. Thank you for the air I breathed, for the bread on my table, for the people you placed on my path and for every moment I felt your kindness, even when I did not realize it. Blessed are you, Lord, for you have never left me alone. Thank you also for every prayer you heard today.

Forgive me if, today, I busied myself so much with many things that I forgot to sit at your feet. I acknowledge that weariness and haste rob me of peace. Teach me to choose what is best: Your presence, your word and the calm that you alone can give. I give you my pending tasks, my worries and everything I could not finish. Tomorrow will be another day and you already hold it in your hands. Teach me to serve without losing joy.

Watch tonight over my family and my loved ones. Console the sick, accompany those who are alone and give rest to those who cannot sleep. May my sleep be peaceful and may I wake renewed, with a desire to serve you. Thank you for your love that never fails and for your peace that surpasses all understanding. In Jesus’ name, amen.

📚 Today’s Story

In the village of Bethany lived three siblings who were friends of Jesus: Martha, Mary and Lazarus. The evangelist Luke relates that, while Jesus was on the road with his disciples, a woman named Martha welcomed him into her home. She was a generous hostess, who wanted everything to be in order to honor her guest.

While Martha was occupied with the preparations, her sister Mary did something different: she sat at Jesus’ feet and listened to his word. This was no trivial gesture, for sitting at the teacher’s feet was the posture of a disciple who wants to learn. Martha, increasingly tired and burdened with serving, came to Jesus and asked whether he did not care that her sister had left her to do all the serving alone, and begged him to tell her to help.

The Lord’s answer was full of tenderness. He said to her: “Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things, but one thing is necessary. Mary has chosen the good portion, which will not be taken away from her.” Jesus did not rebuke her for serving, for service is beautiful. What he did was show her that her busyness had taken her away from what matters most. Notice that he called her by name twice, as one speaks with affection to someone one knows very well.

This scene teaches us that God does not want us to live running and overwhelmed, but to learn to stop beside him. Work has its place, but the relationship with the Lord is the foundation of all the rest. At the end of the day, like Martha, we can lay our rushing at his feet and receive the calm that he alone gives.

✝️ Today’s Message

It is very easy to resemble Martha. We all have lists of tasks, people to serve and things to settle. There is nothing wrong with working diligently; Jesus did not criticize Martha’s service. What he noticed was her troubled heart. When work takes our peace, something needs adjusting, and the solution is not to stop serving, but to sit down again beside the Lord.

The psalm invites us to bless the Lord by night. The night is a gift, for it allows us to close the door on the rush and look upward. You do not need to have finished everything in order to give thanks. It is enough to lift your empty hands and say: “Lord, today I did what I could; now I rest in you.” This simple prayer is worth more than a thousand frettings.

Notice also that Jesus called Martha by name twice. God does not see you as a machine that produces results, but as a beloved child. He knows your weariness, your worries and also your good desires. There is no reproach in his voice, but an invitation: come, sit down, listen and rest. The good portion he offers will not be taken from you by any problem.

Before sleeping, do these three things. Write on a piece of paper what worries you about tomorrow and give it to God in prayer. Then give thanks for three good things that happened today, however small. Finally, read a verse in silence and let its message accompany you until sleep overtakes you. In this way, your night will be a time of rest and not of anxiety.

As you close this day, place in the Lord’s hands everything you could not settle. He watches over you while you sleep and never grows tired. Rest in peace, with the assurance that his mercy covers you like a warm blanket. May you rise full of new strength.
