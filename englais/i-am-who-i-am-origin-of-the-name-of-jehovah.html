I Am Who I Am: The Origin of the Name Jehovah

The burning bush and the revelation of the name · Bible Word God

A Bush That Burned Without Being Consumed

The story begins in the wilderness of Midian. Moses, who had been a shepherd for forty years, saw a bush that burned without being consumed. He drew near to look and heard the voice of God calling him by name. There, on holy ground, God entrusted him with an impossible mission: to return to Egypt and bring his people out of slavery (Exodus 3:1-10).

Faced with such a task, Moses made excuses. But he asked a question that reveals something beautiful to us: he wanted to know God’s name.

Moses’ Question

Moses told God that if he went to the Israelites and told them that the God of their fathers had sent him, they would ask: what is his name? What shall I tell them? For the Hebrews, a name was not a mere label, but expressed the character and essence of the person. Moses wanted to be able to present God clearly to a bruised people.

God was not offended by the question. He answered with one of the most profound sentences in all of Scripture.

“I Am Who I Am”

Exodus 3:14“God said to Moses: I am who I am. And he said: This is what you shall say to the people of Israel: ‘I Am’ has sent me to you.”

In Hebrew, the expression “Ehyeh asher ehyeh” is linked to the verb “to be.” It can be understood as “I am who I am,” “I will be who I will be” or “I will be with you.” All these readings enrich the meaning.

God presents himself as the Being who exists by himself, depending on no one, and who at the same time promises his presence. This is not an invented or limited god, but the origin of everything that exists.

From “I Am” to “Jehovah”

Exodus 3:15“God also said to Moses: You shall say this to the people of Israel: Jehovah, the God of your fathers, the God of Abraham, the God of Isaac and the God of Jacob, has sent me to you. This is my name forever, and this is my name from generation to generation.”

The name YHWH is built on the same verb “to be.” If “Ehyeh” is “I am” in the first person, “Yahweh” is “He is,” the form in which others speak of him. When God says “I am,” we say “He is.” In this way the revelation of the name and the revelation of character are united.

A Name Tied to a Promise

This name was not given in a theoretical lesson, but in the midst of suffering. God said that he had seen the affliction of his people, had heard their cry and knew their sorrows (Exodus 3:7). His name is tied to his compassion and his saving power.

In Exodus 6, God adds that to the patriarchs he had not made himself fully known by his name Jehovah, but that now he would do so by fulfilling his covenant (Exodus 6:3). To know the name is to experience his faithfulness.

Jesus and the “I Am”

In the Gospel of John, Jesus uses the expression “I am” several times: “I am the bread of life,” “I am the light of the world,” “I am the good shepherd,” “I am the resurrection and the life.” And once he declared: “Before Abraham was, I am” (John 8:58). His hearers understood what he was saying and picked up stones to stone him, because they recognized the allusion to God’s name.

Thus, the “I am” of the bush is fully revealed in Christ, the God who comes to save.

What This Means for You

The God who presented himself as “I am” is still the same. When you feel that your past condemns you, he is. When you fear the future, he is. When you do not know how to go on, he is. He is limited neither by time nor by your circumstances.

We invite you to read all of Exodus 3 and to pray like this: “Lord, you are the one who is. I trust in you.” There is a very deep peace in knowing that the center of everything is not you, but an eternal God who loves you and is with you.
