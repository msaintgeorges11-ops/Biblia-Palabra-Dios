📖 Book of Colossians

Christ Is Supreme · 4 chapters

Context

Paul wrote this letter from prison to the believers in Colossae, a city in the Lycus valley, in present-day Turkey. The church had been born from the preaching of Epaphras, a co-worker of Paul who visited him to inform him of its situation. Epaphras told him of the Colossians' faith and love, but also of certain teachings that threatened their faith: a mixture of human philosophy, rituals, worship of angels and ascetic rules that took away from Christ the place that is rightfully his.

Central Message

The central message is the supremacy of Christ. Paul presents him as the image of the invisible God, the firstborn of all creation, the Creator and sustainer of the universe, the head of the Church and the reconciler of all things. In him all the fullness of deity dwells bodily and in him we are made full. That is why the believer does not need to add philosophies, rituals or special experiences: everything he needs, he has in Christ, and it is from this fullness that he must live a new life.

Structure of the Book

  • Chapter 1: prayer of thanks, hymn to the supremacy of Christ and Paul's ministry.
  • Chapter 2: the fullness in Christ and the warning against human philosophies and rules.
  • Chapter 3: putting off the old self, putting on the new and relationships within the home.
  • Chapter 4: instructions on prayer, wisdom toward outsiders and the final greetings.

Main Themes

The first theme is Christ above all things. Paul does not see Jesus as one teacher among others or one mediator among others, but as the center of creation and redemption. By his blood shed on the cross, we have been reconciled with God. If Christ holds first place in the universe, he must also hold it in personal life and in the life of the Church.

The second theme is the resurrected life. Paul affirms that believers have died and been raised with Christ, and that, for this reason, they must seek the things above, where Christ is seated at the right hand of God. This implies giving up anger, malice, lying and immorality, and putting on compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, patience and, above all, love, which is the perfect bond.

The third theme is that faith is lived out daily. Paul gives instructions to husbands, wives, children, fathers and servants, and asks them to do everything heartily, as for the Lord and not for men. He also encourages them to persevere in prayer with thanksgiving and to speak with grace and wisdom, seasoning their words with salt, so as to know how to answer each person.

Key Verses

  • Colossians 1:15-17
  • Colossians 2:9-10
  • Colossians 3:1-2
  • Colossians 3:23

Reflecting on the Verses

In Colossians 1:15-17, Paul exalts Christ as Creator and sustainer of all things. In Colossians 2:9-10, he affirms that in him dwells the fullness of God and that in him we too are made full. In Colossians 3:1-2, he invites us to set our minds on things above and not on those of earth. And in Colossians 3:23, he teaches us that all work, however humble, gains value when it is done heartily for the Lord.

Application for Today

Colossians helps us ask what place Christ holds in our priorities. Sometimes we seek security in opinions, fashionable ideas or experiences, and we forget that he is enough. It also encourages us to give dignity to our daily work: the office, the home, the workshop or our studies can become places of worship when we do them for the Lord and with integrity.

Questions to Reflect On

  • What things compete with Christ for first place in my life?
  • What habit of the old self do I need to give up with God's help?
  • How can I do my work this week as for the Lord?

Prayer

Lord Jesus, you are supreme over all things. Take first place in my heart, help me to live as one raised with you and to do everything for your glory. Amen.
