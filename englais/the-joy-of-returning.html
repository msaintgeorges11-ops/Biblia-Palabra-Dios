Tonight

Theme: The Joy of Returning

The Prodigal Son

⏱ Approximate duration: 5 min

📖 Today’s Verses

Luke 15:20-24
Isaiah 55:7

🙏 Today’s Prayer

Good Father, at the end of this day, we come to give you thanks. You have waited for us with patience, like the father who watches the road every evening. Thank you, because you do not treat us according to our sins and, when we return to you, you run to welcome us. Your love is greater than our faults and your forgiveness wider than our shame.

We acknowledge that today, at one moment or another, we strayed from your will. Perhaps through a harsh word, a proud thought, or an omission that could have helped someone. We ask your forgiveness. Thank you, because you abundantly pardon and because in Christ there is no condemnation for those who trust in him. Cleanse us and renew in us a grateful heart.

Tonight we entrust to you all those who have wandered from you and from their homes. Bring back the children who are far away, heal broken relationships, and teach us to welcome with joy the one who returns. Take rancor from us and make us merciful as you are merciful. We rest on your grace and sleep in your peace, knowing that tomorrow your compassions will be new. Thank you for loving us as we are. In Jesus’ name, amen.

📚 Today’s Story

Jesus told a parable about a father who had two sons. The younger asked for his share of the inheritance, went off to a far country, and squandered all his money there, living unwisely. When a great famine arose, he began to be in need. He ended up feeding pigs and longing to be filled with the food they ate.

Then, Jesus said, he came to himself. He remembered that in his father’s house even the hired servants had bread in abundance. He decided to go back and prepared a few words: “Father, I have sinned against heaven and before you; I am no longer worthy to be called your son; treat me as one of your hired servants.”

But the father saw him while he was still far off. He was moved with compassion, ran, threw his arms around his neck, and kissed him. The son began his speech, but the father did not even let him finish. He ordered his servants to bring the best robe, a ring for his hand, and sandals for his feet. He had the fattened calf killed for a feast, for, he said, “this my son was dead, and is alive again; he was lost, and is found.”

Jesus told this story to show us what God is like. He is not a judge waiting with arms crossed, but a Father who watches the road and runs to meet us. The door is never closed to the one who decides to return. The prophet Isaiah had announced it: let the wicked forsake his way and return to the Lord, who will have mercy on him, for he will abundantly pardon. It was the greatest of all Jesus’ parables about mercy, and those who heard it for the first time understood that the heart of God rejoices more over a homecoming than over anything else in the world.

✝️ Today’s Message

This parable speaks to us whether we have been the son who left or the one who stayed at home. At nightfall, it is a good time to ask ourselves: is there something today that distances me from my Father’s heart? God does not seek our perfection, he seeks our return.

First, come to yourself, as the young man did. Spend a few minutes in silence and review your day honestly. If there was a word or action that did not please God, acknowledge it. No long speech is needed. The son had prepared one, but the father welcomed him before he finished it. Forgiveness awaits us even before we have finished asking for it.

Second, accept the embrace. Many believers return to God but go on feeling like hired servants. The father restored his son’s place: robe, ring, and sandals. If God has forgiven you, stop punishing yourself. Receiving his grace is also a way of honoring him.

Third, imitate the father. Perhaps someone in your family or church needs a gesture of forgiveness from you today. Write a message, make a call, or at the very least pray for that person. And before you sleep, give thanks, because there is joy in heaven over every sinner who repents. Sleep in peace: you are loved as a son or daughter. May this assurance go with you as you close your eyes. God never tires of welcoming you, and his door is open at every hour of the night.

Thank you, Father, for one more day under your care. Hand over tonight your burdens and your faults, for his grace covers them. Rest quietly in the embrace of the one who never ceases to love you. May your sleep be sweet and your heart at peace.
