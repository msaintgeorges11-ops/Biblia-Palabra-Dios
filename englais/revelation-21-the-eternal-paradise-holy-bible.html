📚 Revelation 21 The Eternal Paradise - Holy Bible

The final destiny of God's children · Bible Word God

God with his people

John writes: "Behold, the tabernacle of God is with men, and he will dwell with them, and they shall be his people" (Revelation 21:3). The greatest promise of paradise is not a place, but the presence of God with us.

The end of suffering

"And God shall wipe away all tears from their eyes; and there shall be no more death, neither sorrow, nor crying, neither shall there be any more pain" (v. 4). If you are grieving a loss or an illness today, this is one of the most beautiful promises in the Bible.

A city of light

The new Jerusalem has no need of the sun or the moon, for the glory of God lights it (v. 23). In chapter 22 the river of the water of life flows, and the tree of life grows.

Whoever is thirsty will receive freely from the spring of the water of life (v. 6). That invitation is still open today.

The context of the book

Revelation was written by John, exiled on the island of Patmos, to Christians who were suffering persecution. Its message is one of encouragement: even if evil seems powerful, Christ is victorious. Chapters 21 and 22 are its culmination: after the defeat of evil, God makes all things new.

It is significant that the Bible ends this way. It does not end with destruction, but with restoration. The garden of Genesis returns in the form of a garden city, and what was lost in Eden is found again in Christ.

What will no longer exist

Verses 4 and 8 list what disappears: death, mourning, crying, pain, and everything that is a curse (22:3). For anyone who has known illness, loss or injustice, these words are a balm. God does not minimize our pain; he knows it and promises to remove it.

Moreover, "nothing unclean" will enter the city (21:27). Evil will have no place there. And those who enter are those whose names are written in the Lamb's book of life, that is, those who put their trust in Christ.

What there will be

There will be communion with God: "they shall see his face" (22:4). There will be light, life and healing: the leaves of the tree of life are "for the healing of the nations" (22:2). There will be people of every nation, tribe and language gathered together (7:9). And there will be rest and complete joy.

The image of gates of pearl and streets of gold expresses its immense value. More than the splendor, what matters is that God is present. Verse 22 says there is no temple, because the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are its temple.

An invitation at the end of the Bible

Revelation ends with an invitation: "And the Spirit and the bride say, Come. And let him that heareth say, Come. And let him that is athirst come. And whosoever will, let him take the water of life freely" (22:17). This invitation is for you today. There is nothing to pay or to earn: only to receive.

While you wait for that day, live with hope. Care for those who suffer, pray for those who do not know Christ, and keep trusting. The last verse says: "The grace of our Lord Jesus Christ be with you all. Amen." (22:21).

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to understand all of Revelation to receive hope? No. Christians of every era have read these chapters as a comfort. Even if the symbols are difficult, the message is clear: God prevails and renews all things.

How should I read it? Start with chapters 21 and 22, then read chapters 1 to 3. Ask God for understanding and consult a study Bible. Revelation 1:3 promises a blessing to the one who reads and keeps its words.

To reflect on this week

Spend a few minutes each day imagining God's promise, and let that hope bring you peace in your current concerns.

Suggested verses: Revelation 21:1-5, Revelation 22:1-5, Isaiah 25:8, Romans 8:18 and 2 Corinthians 4:17-18. Read one each day, ask yourself what it teaches you about God and what concrete step you can take. Finish with a short prayer, giving thanks for what you have learned and asking for help to put it into practice. Sharing what you discover with a loved one or a friend helps you remember it and may encourage others.

Prayer

Father, thank you for the promise of a world without tears. Sustain my hope until I arrive home. In Jesus' name, amen.
