Theme: God’s Invisible Armies

Elisha and the Heavenly Army at Dothan

⏱ Approximate duration: 5 min

📖 Today’s Verses

2 Kings 6:15-17
1 Peter 5:7

🙏 Today’s Prayer

Good Father, at the end of this day, I come to you with a grateful heart. Thank you for keeping me, for providing what I needed, and for every moment when I felt your kindness, even if I did not recognize it at the time. Your faithfulness has accompanied me from morning to evening.

I confess that today there were moments when I looked only at what was in front of me and let fear carry me away. Open the eyes of my heart, Lord, as you opened the eyes of Elisha’s servant, to see that those who are with me are more than those who are against me. I cast all my anxiety on you, because you care for me. I hand over to you my unfinished tasks, my worries about tomorrow, and the things I cannot control. Thank you for the people who helped me today, for the small joys I sometimes let slip by, and for the fact that your love does not depend on my successes.

Take care of my family while we sleep and watch over our homes. Heal what is sick, comfort those who weep, and strengthen those who feel alone. Give me deep sleep and a hope-filled awakening. Thank you, because you never leave me nor forsake me. In Jesus’ name, amen.

📚 Today’s Story

Elisha was a prophet in Israel, and God often revealed to him the plans of the king of Syria when he set ambushes against Israel. The king of Syria, furious, discovered that it was the prophet who was warning them, and he sent horses, chariots, and a great army to the city of Dothan, where Elisha was. They arrived by night and surrounded the city.

When Elisha’s servant got up early in the morning and went out, he saw the army with its horses and chariots all around the city. Terrified, he ran to tell his master: Alas, my lord! What shall we do? Humanly speaking, the situation was hopeless.

Elisha answered calmly: Do not be afraid, for those who are with us are more than those who are with them. And he prayed: Lord, open his eyes, that he may see. The Lord opened the servant’s eyes, and he saw that the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire all around Elisha.

When the servant’s eyes were opened, fear gave way to calm. This story, written in the second book of Kings, has comforted believers for centuries, because it reminds us that the one who is in danger is not alone. God’s presence is real even if our eyes do not always perceive it, and his resources exceed every threat we face. God cares for his people in ways we cannot even imagine.

What changed was not the visible situation, but the servant’s ability to see the whole reality. God had been present with his protection all along. From the beginning, the heavenly army surrounded the prophet, and this young man only needed God to open his eyes to behold it.

✝️ Today’s Message

How often we end the day exhausted because we have looked only at the army surrounding us: debts, bad news, conflicts, uncertainty. Elisha’s servant was not wrong about what he saw; the Syrian army was real. His mistake was thinking that was all there was.

The Bible invites us to see further. This does not mean denying problems, but remembering that God is present too and that his protection is greater than any threat. Peter puts it simply: cast all your anxiety on him, because he cares for you. He does not say to forget the worry, but to cast it on God, like someone setting a heavy burden into stronger hands.

Tonight, do a simple exercise. Think of the situation that worries you most and, in prayer, describe it to God in your own words. Then say: Lord, open my eyes to see your goodness. Next, remember three moments from the day when God helped you, even small ones: a kind word, a door that opened, a problem that got resolved.

Finally, let go of what you cannot control. God does not ask you to solve everything before you sleep; he asks you to trust him. Lie down in peace, knowing that, as at Dothan, there is more from God on your side than what you see. And tomorrow, when you wake, begin again with gratitude.

Rest tonight, for God watches over you with more care than you imagine. Place every burden in his hands and let his peace guard your heart. Tomorrow will find you once more with his goodness and faithfulness. May your heart fill with gratitude as you close your eyes.
