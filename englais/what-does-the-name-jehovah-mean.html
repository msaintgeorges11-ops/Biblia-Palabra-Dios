What Does the Name Jehovah Mean?

God’s personal name and its meaning · Bible Word God

A Name, Not Just a Title

When the Bible speaks of God, it does not leave him anonymous. Alongside titles such as “God,” “Lord” or “Most High,” Scripture presents a personal name that recurs constantly from Genesis to Malachi. In many English Bibles, this name is written “Jehovah.” Understanding what it means helps us know better the one who created us and approach him with more confidence.

A person may hold an office, but their name is what identifies them and what makes a close relationship possible. The same is true of God: the name reveals that he is neither a distant force nor an abstract idea, but a personal Being who desires to be known, called upon and loved.

Where the Word Jehovah Comes From

In the original Hebrew language, God’s name is written with four consonants: YHWH. It is called the Tetragrammaton, which means “four letters.” Ancient Hebrew was written only with consonants, and readers filled in the vowels from memory. In time, out of reverence, people stopped pronouncing the name aloud, and the exact original pronunciation was lost.

The form “Jehovah” was born centuries later, when the copyists joined the consonants YHWH to the vowels of the word “Adonai” (Lord). From this came the reading Yehovah, which passed into English as Jehovah. Many scholars today prefer the form “Yahweh” as closer to the ancient sound, but “Jehovah” has a long history in translations and hymns, and millions of believers use it with respect and affection.

The Meaning of the Name

The name YHWH is linked to the Hebrew verb “to be” or “to exist.” It is therefore generally understood as “He is,” “He exists” or “The One who is.” When Moses asked God how he should introduce him to the people, God answered: “I AM THAT I AM” (Exodus 3:14). And he added that the name Jehovah, the God of his fathers Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, was his name forever (Exodus 3:15).

This meaning holds beautiful truths. God exists by himself, with no beginning and no end. He depends on no one. He is present, he acts and he fulfills what he promises. That is why the name Jehovah also conveys faithfulness: the God who was with the patriarchs is the same one who walks with us today.

What the Name Teaches Us About God

First, it teaches us that God is eternal and unchanging. The prophet Malachi puts it clearly: “I am the LORD, I change not” (Malachi 3:6). In a world where everything changes, knowing that God remains firm is an anchor for the soul.

Second, it teaches us that God is unique. “Hear, O Israel: The LORD our God is one LORD” (Deuteronomy 6:4). There is no other like him, and no other deserves our supreme worship.

Third, it teaches us that God is near. The one who reveals his name lets himself be reached. Throughout the Bible, those who cried out “Jehovah” found an attentive ear: Abraham, Moses, Hannah, David, Elijah, Hezekiah and many others.

God’s Name and Jesus Christ

In the New Testament, Jesus said in his prayer: “I have manifested thy name unto the men which thou gavest me out of the world” (John 17:6). Christ did not come only to teach doctrines, but to show who the Father is: compassionate, just, patient and full of grace. Whoever sees Jesus sees what God is like. And Scripture declares that Jesus is Lord, to the glory of God the Father (Philippians 2:11).

Thus, knowing God’s name is not limited to a historical or linguistic fact. It leads us to a living relationship, which grows stronger as we read his Word, pray sincerely and obey what he teaches.

How to Use This Knowledge Every Day

You can begin simply. When you are afraid, remember that the God who says “I am” is with you. When you feel alone, remember that he has a name and calls you by yours. When you have to make a decision, remember that the one who has existed forever knows the end from the beginning.

We invite you to calmly read the passages listed at the end of this article and to speak to God in prayer. You do not need complicated words. A sincere heart that says, “Jehovah, I want to know you,” is enough. He has promised to let himself be found by those who seek him with all their heart.

  • Deuteronomy 6:4 · Jehovah is the only one
