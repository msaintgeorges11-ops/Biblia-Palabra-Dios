Theme: Calm in the Storm

Jesus Calms the Storm

⏱ Approximate duration: 6 min

📖 Today’s Verses

Matthew 8:23-27
Psalm 56:3-4

🙏 Today’s Prayer

Heavenly Father, I thank you for this new day you have set before me. Before the rush and the worries begin, I want to pause for a moment and acknowledge that you are my Lord, my refuge, and my strength. You kept me through the night and woke me with life and with hope. Everything I have comes from your generous hand.

Today, Lord, contrary winds may blow: news I did not expect, tasks that weigh on me, and situations that make me doubt. I confess that sometimes I am filled with fear and it seems the boat is sinking. But you are not far from me. Teach me to trust you when the sea is stirred, and to remember that a single word from you is enough to bring calm. Take from my heart the fear that paralyzes, and give me a simple, steady faith.

Guide my steps, my words, and my decisions throughout this day. May I speak kindly, work diligently, and rest in your care. When I feel weak, remind me that you are with me in the same boat. Fill my home with peace and protect the people I love. I give you this entire day, with everything it brings, trusting that you will do what is best. In Jesus’ name, amen.

📚 Today’s Story

That evening, by the Sea of Galilee, Jesus had tended to a great many people and was tired. Then he gave his disciples a simple instruction: let us go over to the other side of the lake. They got into the boat with him and began to sail. Among them were fishermen who knew these waters well, and nothing suggested the trip would be difficult.

Suddenly a storm so great arose on the sea that the waves covered the boat. Those men, used to the lake, were filled with fear. Meanwhile, Jesus was asleep. He was not absent or indifferent; he was exhausted, and he was with them in the same boat. In desperation they woke him and said: Lord, save us, we are perishing.

Jesus answered them: Why are you afraid, you of little faith? Then he rose, rebuked the winds and the sea, and there was a great calm. The disciples marveled and said to one another: What kind of man is this, that even the winds and the sea obey him? This scene is found in chapter 8 of the Gospel of Matthew.

Notice a very important detail: the storm came while the disciples were obeying Jesus. Being on God’s path does not spare us from storms, but it assures us that the Master goes with us. The disciples had to learn that Jesus’ presence in the boat is greater than any wave. They also learned that calling out to him, even with little faith, is never in vain. He listens, rises, and acts at just the right moment, and his voice has authority over everything that seems impossible for us to control.

✝️ Today’s Message

Fear is one of the most human emotions. The disciples felt it even though they were right beside Jesus, and so do we. It can come as worry about health, work, family, or the future. The good news is that the Bible does not scold us for feeling afraid; it teaches us what to do with it. The psalmist David wrote: When I am afraid, I will trust in you. Notice that he does not say he would never be afraid, but that he chose to trust.

Trust begins when we bring our fear to Jesus, just as the disciples did. They woke him and told him the truth: we are perishing. We do not need to pretend before God that we are strong. We can honestly say to him: Lord, I am afraid, help me. He does not mock our weakness; on the contrary, he draws near. And although Jesus pointed out their little faith, he did not abandon them. He gave them calm and showed them who he was.

For today, I suggest something practical. Think of the situation that troubles you most and put it into a short prayer, in your own words. Then read Psalm 56, verses 3 and 4, aloud, and slowly repeat: In God I have put my trust; I will not fear. If fear returns during the day, repeat that phrase. Taking a deep breath and remembering God’s word is a simple way to hand over the burden again and again.

Also, try to be someone who brings calm to others. Perhaps someone around you is going through their own storm. A kind word, a phone call, or a moment to listen can be like a small ray of peace. Jesus calmed the sea with a word, and he calls us to be carriers of his peace. Go out today with the assurance that you are not sailing alone: the one who commands the winds is in the boat with you.

May you walk through this day with the certainty that Jesus is with you in the boat. When the strong wind comes, remember that his word brings calm. Go out with peace and courage, because the Lord watches over you every hour.
