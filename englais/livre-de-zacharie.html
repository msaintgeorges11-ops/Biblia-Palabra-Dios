📖 Book of Zechariah

Your King comes to you · 14 chapters

Background

Zechariah, son of Berechiah and grandson of Iddo, from a priestly family, prophesied in Jerusalem beginning in 520 BC, shortly after Haggai, to encourage those who had returned from exile to rebuild the temple. The people were discouraged and small in the face of great empires. The first chapters contain night visions, and the last ones, messages about a future king and the day of the Lord.

Central message

The central message is that God will return to Jerusalem with mercy and fulfill his promises, not by human strength but by his Spirit. He calls the people to return to him and announces the humble King who will enter riding on a donkey, the shepherd who is struck, the fountain opened to cleanse from sin, and the day when the Lord will be king over all the earth.

Structure of the book

  • Chapter 1:1 to 6: the call to return to the Lord and the warning not to repeat the mistake of the fathers.
  • Chapters 1:7 to 6:8: the eight night visions, such as the man among the myrtles, the lampstand, and the four chariots.
  • Chapters 6:9 to 8: the crown for Joshua, the question about fasting, and the promises of Jerusalem's restoration.
  • Chapters 9 to 11: the coming of the humble King, the rejected shepherd, and the thirty pieces of silver.
  • Chapters 12 to 14: the spirit of grace, the fountain for sin, and the day when the Lord will reign.

Main themes

The first theme is the power of the Spirit. To Zerubbabel, who faced a mountain of difficulties, it is said that the work will be done not by an army or by might, but by the Spirit of the Lord. This word encourages those who feel few and weak. God accomplishes his plans through humble people who depend on him, and he removes obstacles like a mountain that becomes a plain.

The second theme is the Messiah who comes. Zechariah announces the righteous and humble King, on a donkey, who will bring peace to the nations, a fulfillment that the Gospels see in Jesus' entry into Jerusalem. He also speaks of the shepherd betrayed for thirty pieces, and of the one they have pierced and over whom they will mourn. There is a clear shadow of the cross.

The third theme is purification and the future kingdom. God will open a fountain for sin and uncleanness, and the day will come when he will be one, and his name one. The book leads us from discouragement to triumph, and assures us that history ends with God reigning. In the meantime, it invites us to return to him, who promises to dwell in the midst of his people.

Key verses

  • Zechariah 4:6
  • Zechariah 9:9
  • Zechariah 12:10
  • Zechariah 14:9

Reflecting on the verses

In Zechariah 4:6, it is affirmed that the work is accomplished by the Spirit of the Lord and not by human power. Zechariah 9:9 calls Jerusalem to rejoice because her King comes, righteous, a savior, and humble. In Zechariah 12:10, the spirit of grace is announced, and the mourning over the one they have pierced. And Zechariah 14:9 declares that the Lord will be king over all the earth.

Application for today

Zechariah encourages us not to despise the day of small things. If we face projects that are beyond us, we can ask for the Spirit's help instead of relying only on our own resources. We can also thank the humble King who has come every day, and wait joyfully for his return. In the meantime, we live with hope and serve with simplicity. Every small act of obedience, done in faith, is part of what God is building, and he does not despise these modest beginnings.

Questions for reflection

  • What mountain of difficulty do I need to hand over to the Spirit of God?
  • How does the humble King who entered riding on a donkey speak to me today?
  • What changes in my life when I know that one day the Lord will reign over all the earth?

Prayer

Humble King and Savior, thank you for coming to me with justice and peace. Give me your Spirit to do your work without relying on my own strength, and help me to wait for your kingdom with joy. Amen.
