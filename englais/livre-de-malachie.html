📖 Book of Malachi

The messenger before the Lord · 4 chapters

Background

Malachi, whose name means my messenger, is the last of the prophets of the Old Testament. He prophesied in Jerusalem around the 5th century BC, probably after the rebuilding of the temple and at a time close to Nehemiah. The Jews had returned from exile, but they were disillusioned: they doubted God's love, offered defective animals, the priests were negligent, and there were divorces, injustices, and little generosity.

Central message

The central message is that God loves his people and asks them for sincere faithfulness. Through questions and answers, the Lord corrects the contempt for his name, the offering of the worst, marital unfaithfulness, the lack of tithes, and the complaints against his justice. He announces that he will send his messenger and that the Lord will come to his temple, with the sun of righteousness bringing healing to those who fear him.

Structure of the book

  • Chapter 1:1 to 5: God's love for Israel, which the people call into question with their query.
  • Chapter 1:6 to 2:9: the rebuke to the priests for dishonest offerings and for turning aside from the way.
  • Chapter 2:10 to 17: unfaithfulness to the covenant, broken marriages, and God's weariness at their words.
  • Chapter 3:1 to 18: the messenger who prepares the way, the refiner, the tithes, and the difference between the righteous and the wicked.
  • Chapter 4: the burning day, the sun of righteousness, the remembrance of the law, and the promise to send Elijah.

Main themes

The first theme is God's love called into question. The book opens with the Lord saying: I have loved you, and the people ask how. It is easy to forget his care when circumstances are not what we expected. God answers by recalling his choice and his faithfulness. The whole book starts from this assurance: God's love precedes our response and sustains it.

The second theme is sincere worship. God rebukes offerings of blind and lame animals, which they would not dare give to a governor, and service performed with boredom. He also calls them to be faithful in marriage and generous with the tithe. Giving God the best is a mark of honor, and he promises to pour out blessing on the one who entrusts him with what he has.

The third theme is the coming of the Lord. God will send his messenger to prepare the way, and then the Lord will come to his temple, like a refining fire. The New Testament sees John the Baptist in the messenger and Jesus in the Lord. The Old Testament ends on an open hope: the sun of righteousness will rise with salvation.

Key verses

  • Malachi 1:2
  • Malachi 3:1
  • Malachi 3:10
  • Malachi 4:2

Reflecting on the verses

In Malachi 1:2, God simply declares that he loves his people. Malachi 3:1 announces the messenger who will prepare the way and the Lord who will suddenly come to his temple. In Malachi 3:10, he invites us to bring the tithes into the storehouse and to test his generosity. And Malachi 4:2 promises that for those who fear his name the sun of righteousness will rise, bringing salvation in its wings.

Application for today

Malachi invites us to check whether we give God the best or only what is left over, of time, money, and attention. Also to remember his love when faith grows cold, and to be faithful in the home. And it prepares us for the Gospel: the promised Messiah has already come, he brings healing, and he will return. In the meantime, we live with reverence, gratitude, and hope.

Questions for reflection

  • Do I give God the best of my time and resources, or what is left over?
  • What evidence of God's love can I remember when I doubt him?
  • How am I preparing to receive the Lord, whose sun of righteousness brings healing?

Prayer

Lord, thank you for loving me first. Forgive my half-hearted offerings and teach me to honor you with the best. Thank you for Jesus, the sun of righteousness, and help me to wait for him faithfully. Amen.
