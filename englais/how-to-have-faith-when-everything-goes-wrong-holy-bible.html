📚 How to Have Faith When Everything Goes Wrong - Holy Bible

Words for those who are suffering · Bible Word God

It is normal to feel worn out

When everything goes wrong, it is natural to have doubts. Job, David and Habakkuk had them too, and the Bible does not hide it. God does not get angry with someone who weeps before him: "Jehovah is near to the brokenhearted" (Psalm 34:18).

Trusting even without seeing

Habakkuk said: "Though the fig tree does not blossom… yet I will rejoice in Jehovah" (Habakkuk 3:17-18). And Paul reminds us: "We walk by faith, not by sight" (2 Corinthians 5:7). Faith is tested when there is no visible sign.

Steps for today

Talk to God with complete honesty. Read a psalm aloud. Look for someone you trust to pray with you. And remember his promise: "Fear not, for I am with you… I will always come to your help" (Isaiah 41:10).

This season is not the end. God is still working, even when you cannot see him.

The example of Job

Job lost everything: his possessions, his children and his health. His friends gave him false explanations, and he himself asked God painful questions. Yet he said: "Even if he kills me, I will hope in him" (Job 13:15). In the end, God restored him, although he never explained everything to him.

What we learn is that we can be honest with God in our pain. He is not offended by our questions. Job did not receive an answer to everything, but he came to know God better.

The psalms of lament

Nearly half of the Psalms are prayers of lament. David writes: "How long, Jehovah, will you forget me forever?" (Psalm 13:1). But almost always they end in trust: "I trust in your goodness" (13:5). The Psalms teach us to bring our emotions before God instead of keeping them to ourselves.

If you do not know how to pray in a crisis, read a psalm aloud. Psalms 23, 27, 34, 42, 46 and 121 are very helpful in those moments.

What God does in the trial

The Bible does not say that suffering is good in itself, but it says that God works in the midst of it. Romans 5:3-4 says that tribulation produces perseverance, perseverance produces proven character, and that proven character produces hope. Peter compares faith to gold purified by fire (1 Peter 1:7).

And Romans 8:28 promises that all things work together for good for those who love God. It does not say that everything is good, but that God can use everything for good.

Concrete actions

When everything goes wrong, it helps to take small steps. Rest as much as you can. Talk to someone you trust; do not carry it all alone. Pray in few words: "Lord, help me." Read a psalm each day. Give thanks for one small thing, even if it is difficult.

If the pain is very strong or you feel you cannot go on, seek help from a pastor, a counselor or a doctor. Asking for help is not a lack of faith. God often supports us through other people. And remember: "Cast all your cares on him, because he cares for you" (1 Peter 5:7).

Frequently asked questions

Is it a sin to be angry with God? The psalmists expressed their anger and confusion in prayer. God prefers honesty to silence. What matters is to keep talking to him.

When should I seek professional help? If the sadness lasts for weeks, or affects your sleep, your appetite or your desire to live, seek medical and psychological help. God also works through doctors and counselors.

To reflect on this week

On difficult days, read short passages. Do not demand too much of yourself; let God sustain you while you regain your strength.

Suggested verses: Psalm 34:18, Psalm 46:1-3, Isaiah 41:10, Matthew 11:28-30 and 1 Peter 5:7. Read one each day, ask yourself what it teaches you about God and what concrete step you can take. Finish with a short prayer, giving thanks for what you have learned and asking for help to put it into practice. Sharing what you discover with a loved one or a friend helps you remember it and may encourage others.

Prayer

Lord, I am tired, but I trust in you. Sustain me today and renew my strength. In Jesus' name, amen.
