The 7 Names of Jehovah: Jireh, Rapha, Nissi

Seven ways God reveals himself to his people · Bible Word God

A God Who Reveals Himself Through His Character

Throughout the Old Testament, the name Jehovah appears joined to other words that describe how God acts at specific moments. Christian tradition usually gathers seven of these combinations: Jehovah Jireh, Jehovah Rapha, Jehovah Nissi, Jehovah Shalom, Jehovah Rohi, Jehovah Tsidkenu and Jehovah Shammah. These are not seven gods, but seven faces of the same God, who makes himself known according to the need of his people.

We will go through them one by one, with their biblical passage and the lesson they leave us.

Jehovah Jireh: The Lord Will Provide

Abraham gave this name to the place where God provided a ram in place of Isaac: “Jehovah-jireh” (Genesis 22:14). God does not only provide what is necessary, he does it at the right moment, often when we no longer see a way out. It is a name for the one who worries about their livelihood, their work or their family.

God’s provision culminates in Christ, the Lamb whom he himself provided for our salvation.

Jehovah Rapha: The Lord Who Heals

After the crossing of the Red Sea, God said to Israel: “I am the LORD that healeth thee” (Exodus 15:26). The Hebrew word speaks of restoring and caring for. God heals the body, but also the wounded heart, the weary spirit and broken relationships.

This does not mean that every illness always disappears right away, but that we can come to God with complete confidence, knowing that he takes care of us at every step.

Jehovah Nissi: The Lord Is My Banner

After defeating Amalek, Moses built an altar and called it “Jehovah-Nissi,” which means Jehovah is my banner (Exodus 17:15). A banner gathers the army and declares under whose authority it fights. The victory did not come from Israel by its own strength, but from God. It is a name for the one who is facing spiritual or personal battles.

When you feel you can’t go on, remember that the battle is not yours alone: your banner is the Lord.

Jehovah Shalom: The Lord Is Peace

Gideon, frightened at having seen the angel of Jehovah, received this word: “Peace be unto thee; fear not.” He then built an altar and called it “Jehovah-shalom” (Judges 6:24). God’s peace is not the absence of problems, but the assurance of being reconciled with him.

Jesus confirmed it: “Peace I leave with you, my peace I give unto you” (John 14:27). God himself is the source of this peace.

Jehovah Rohi: The Lord Is My Shepherd

David sang: “The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want” (Psalm 23:1). The name “Rohi” expresses care, guidance and companionship. A shepherd knows his sheep, feeds them, defends them and looks for them when they are lost. Jesus presented himself as the good shepherd who lays down his life for his sheep.

If you feel you have lost your way, this name reminds you that God knows where you are and calls you by name.

Jehovah Tsidkenu and Jehovah Shammah

The prophet Jeremiah announced a King whose name would be “THE LORD OUR RIGHTEOUSNESS” (Jeremiah 23:6). We are not righteous by our works, but by the righteousness God grants us in Christ. It is a name of hope for those who carry guilt.

Finally, Ezekiel ends his vision of the city with these words: “Jehovah-Shammah,” that is, “The LORD is there” (Ezekiel 48:35). This is the promise of God’s presence with his people, fulfilled in Jesus, Immanuel, “God with us.”

How to Use These Names in Prayer

Each of these names can become a prayer. When you need provision, say “Jehovah Jireh.” When you are sick, “Jehovah Rapha.” When you face a battle, “Jehovah Nissi.” When there is anguish, “Jehovah Shalom.” When you need guidance, “Jehovah Rohi.” When you carry guilt, “Jehovah Tsidkenu.” When you feel alone, “Jehovah Shammah.”

Remember that these names are not magic formulas. They are windows for knowing God’s character and for trusting that he is faithful to what he reveals about himself.

One Last Reminder

These seven names do not exhaust all that God is, but they give us a map of the heart of Jehovah. He provides, heals, defends, brings peace, leads like a shepherd, justifies and dwells with us. Every human need finds an answer in him. We invite you to choose today the name you need most and to read its passage in full, so that your trust may be strengthened in what God has said about himself. Then give thanks in writing, noting how you have seen the Lord at work in your story.
