📖 Book of 2 John

Truth and Love · 1 chapter

Context

The second letter of John is a brief note written by the elder to the elect lady and her children. It is possible that this refers to a local church with its members, or to a believing woman in whose house the community met. At that time, itinerant teachers traveled from city to city and depended on the hospitality of believers. Some of them taught errors about the person of Christ, which is why John writes to warn clearly without ever setting love aside.

Central Message

The central message is that truth and love must always go together. John rejoices to see that some of the lady's children are walking in the truth, and he reminds them of the commandment they have had from the beginning: that we love one another. But he also warns against deceivers who do not confess that Jesus Christ has come in the flesh. Loving does not mean accepting any teaching, and defending the truth does not justify a lack of love.

Structure of the Book

  • Verses 1 to 3: the elder's greeting to the elect lady and her children, with grace, mercy and peace.
  • Verses 4 to 6: the joy over those who walk in the truth and the commandment of love.
  • Verses 7 to 11: the warning against deceivers and the limit of hospitality.
  • Verses 12 and 13: the desire to visit them and the final greetings.

Main Themes

The first theme is walking in the truth. John mentions the truth several times in a few lines: he loves in the truth, the truth abides in us and we walk in the truth. For him, truth is not just a set of correct ideas, but a person, Jesus Christ, and a way of living. Whoever knows the truth is free and sees his life transformed by it.

The second theme is love that obeys. John recalls that love means walking according to God's commandments. There is no opposition between loving and obeying: love is expressed in obedience, and obedience is born from love. This teaching protects us from sentimental love without commitment and from cold obedience without affection.

The third theme is discernment in hospitality. John asks us not to receive into the house or greet anyone who comes with a teaching that denies Christ, for whoever does so shares in his wicked works. This does not mean treating with contempt those who think differently, but not offering a platform or support to those who spread doctrines that destroy faith. Wisdom is needed to balance grace and truth.

Key Verses

  • 2 John 3
  • 2 John 4
  • 2 John 6
  • 2 John 9

Reflecting on the Verses

In 2 John 3, the apostle wishes that grace, mercy and peace be with his readers in truth and love. In 2 John 4, he expresses his joy at seeing the lady's children walking in the truth. In 2 John 6, he defines love in terms of obedience to God's commandments. And in 2 John 9, he warns that whoever abides in the teaching of Christ has both the Father and the Son.

Application for Today

This little letter invites us to balance conviction and compassion. We can love people without giving up the truth, and defend the truth without ceasing to love. It also reminds us that we must know well what we believe, so as not to be carried away by every novelty. And it encourages us to make our homes places where the truth of Christ is taught, lived and passed on.

Questions to Reflect On

  • How can I better unite love and truth in my conversations?
  • Do I know the Bible well enough to discern what I hear?
  • Is my home a place where the truth of Christ is honored?

Prayer

Lord, you are truth and love. Keep me from all error, teach me to obey you with joy and help me to love others without giving up your truth. Amen.
