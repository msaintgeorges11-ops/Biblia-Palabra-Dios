The Name of Jehovah in the 66 Books of the Bible

From Genesis to Revelation, one God revealing himself · Bible Word God

One God, One Bible

The Bible brings together sixty-six books, written by about forty authors over fifteen hundred years. And yet all of them speak of the same God. In this article, we look at how the name of Jehovah appears throughout Scripture, staying honest about what the texts say: the name appears explicitly in the Old Testament and is reflected in a different way in the New.

This honesty is important. We do not need to exaggerate anything in order to admire the unity of the Bible: it is impressive enough as it is.

In the Old Testament

In the Hebrew text, the name YHWH appears about 6,800 times, spread over almost every book. It is found in the Law (from Genesis to Deuteronomy), in the historical books, in the poetry and wisdom literature, and in the prophets. Two books, Esther and Ecclesiastes, do not mention it directly, but God’s presence can be perceived in them through his providence and wisdom.

For example, in Genesis it is tied to creation and to the promises (Genesis 2:4). In Exodus, to deliverance (Exodus 3:15). In the Psalms, to praise. In the prophets, to warning and hope.

Between the Two Testaments

The four hundred years that separate Malachi from Matthew are sometimes called the period of silence. During that time, the Jewish people reinforced their reverence for the name, to the point of ceasing to pronounce it. Yet the Hebrew Scriptures were translated into Greek, and in that translation the name was rendered as “Kyrios,” Lord.

This detail helps us understand how the New Testament authors read the Old.

In the New Testament

The Greek manuscripts of the New Testament do not contain the name YHWH in Hebrew. The authors wrote in Greek and used “Lord” and “God.” But in many places, they quote Old Testament texts in which the name appears, and apply them to God the Father or to Jesus Christ.

Jesus spoke of the Father’s name with particular reverence. In his prayer, he said: “I have manifested your name to the people whom you gave me out of the world” (John 17:6). And in the Lord’s Prayer, he taught us to ask: “Hallowed be your name” (Matthew 6:9).

“Hallelujah”: The Name in the Last Book

Revelation offers a magnificent detail. In chapter 19, the heavenly crowd cries out “Hallelujah!” (Revelation 19:1). This word comes from the Hebrew “hallelu-Yah,” which means “praise Yah,” that is, Jehovah. Thus, in the last book of the Bible, God’s name resounds in the worship of heaven.

It is a harmonious conclusion: the name revealed to Moses is praised by redeemed multitudes at the end of the story.

The 66 Books and One Message

From Genesis to Revelation, the common thread is a God who creates, who reveals himself, who redeems and who makes all things new. In the Old Testament, we see him promise; in the New, fulfill in Christ. “All Scripture is breathed out by God” (2 Timothy 3:16), and all its parts bear witness to the same Lord.

This means that every book, however humble, has something to teach us about who God is.

How to Read the Bible in Search of God

We invite you to read the Bible with this question in mind: what does this passage teach me about God? You will see that each book reveals an aspect of his character: his holiness in Leviticus, his faithfulness in Ruth, his wisdom in Proverbs, his love in John.

You can begin today from our reading section. God speaks through his Word, and whoever reads it with an open heart comes to know him ever better.

A Simple Reading Plan

If you want to see the unity of the Bible, you can follow a simple plan: each day read a psalm, a chapter of the Gospels and a chapter of the Old Testament. At the end, write one sentence that answers this question: who is God in this text? After a few weeks, you will notice how the same God reveals himself in narratives, poems, laws and letters, and your faith will grow through regular reading.
