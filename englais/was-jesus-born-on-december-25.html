📅 Was Jesus Really Born on December 25? What History and the Bible Say

The date of Christmas and what really matters

A very common question

Every December, many people wonder whether Jesus was really born on the 25th. The honest answer is that the Bible does not give the date. Neither Matthew nor Luke states the day or the month of the birth. What they do, however, is recount the event accurately: Jesus was born in Bethlehem, to the virgin Mary, to save us.

What the Bible actually says

Luke gives one clue: there were shepherds in the fields, keeping watch over their flocks by night (Luke 2:8). Some scholars think that in the middle of winter in Judea, with the cold and the rains, flocks were generally kept under shelter. Others reply that in the region of Bethlehem, in some years there could still be sheep outdoors in December. So this clue does not settle the question definitively.

We also know that there was a census ordered by Emperor Caesar Augustus (Luke 2:1-3), and that Herod the Great was still reigning at the time of Jesus’ birth (Matthew 2:1). This places the birth a few years before the start of our calendar.

Why do we celebrate on December 25?

The earliest known mention of December 25 as the date of the Nativity appears in the fourth century, in Rome. Historians put forward two main explanations. One says that Christians chose this date to offer a different message in the face of the pagan solstice festivals. The other holds that the date was calculated from an ancient belief that Jesus was conceived on March 25 and born nine months later. Scholars still debate which carries more weight, so it is best to speak about it with humility.

Is it a sin to celebrate Christmas on this date?

The Bible does not command us to celebrate Jesus’ birth on a specific date, but it does not forbid it either. Paul taught that, when it comes to special days, each person should be fully convinced in his own mind, and that whoever observes a day does so for the Lord (Romans 14:5-6). The essential thing is that what we do is to honor God. Each family can decide in prayer how to live out this season.

What really matters

More than finding the right date, what matters is believing in the person. The angel told the shepherds that on that day a Savior had been born (Luke 2:11). John declares that the Word became flesh (John 1:14). The Christian faith does not depend on a calendar, but on a fact: God sent his Son, who was born, died and rose again.

How to make the most of this season

Use the opportunity to talk about Jesus. Many people are more open to God’s message in December.

Avoid arguments. If someone celebrates Christmas differently, respect them with love.

Live Christmas meaningfully. Give thanks, forgive, share and pray as a family.

Read the accounts. The first chapters of Matthew and Luke will give you a solid foundation.

The Lord’s birth and year

Historians estimate that Jesus was born between 6 and 4 BC, because Herod died in 4 BC. This may seem curious, but it takes nothing away from faith. The Gospels are not an almanac; they are a testimony. Luke says he carefully investigated everything from the beginning, so that Theophilus might know the certainty of the things he had been taught (Luke 1:3-4).

A celebration that points to something greater

The date may vary among churches; some Eastern churches celebrate in January. What unites all Christians is the message: God with us. If someone asks you today whether Jesus was born on December 25, you can simply answer: we do not know exactly, but we know why he came and for whom he came.

A prayer for this season

Father, thank you for sending your Son. I do not know the exact day of his birth, but I know he came to save me. Help me to live each day remembering his love, and not only in December. May my life be a constant celebration of your goodness. In Jesus’ name, amen.
