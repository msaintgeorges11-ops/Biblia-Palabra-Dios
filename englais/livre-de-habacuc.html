📖 Book of Habakkuk

The righteous will live by faith · 3 chapters

Background

Habakkuk is a prophet of Judah about whom little is known. According to the text, he prophesied toward the end of the 7th century BC, shortly before the invasion of Babylon, in a time of violence and injustice within the people themselves. Unlike other prophets, the book is not a message addressed to the people, but a dialogue with God, in which the prophet questions, waits, and finally worships.

Central message

The central message is that the righteous will live by faith, even when they do not understand what God is doing. Habakkuk asks why the Lord tolerates evil and why he will use the Chaldeans, even crueler, to punish Judah. God answers that his plan is just and that pride will be judged. The book ends with a prayer of trust: even if everything fails, the prophet will rejoice in the Lord.

Structure of the book

  • Chapter 1:1 to 11: the prophet's first complaint against violence and God's answer, who raises up the Chaldeans.
  • Chapter 1:12 to 2:1: Habakkuk's second complaint about the use of a wicked people, and his decision to wait.
  • Chapter 2:2 to 5: God's answer, who commands him to write the vision and affirms that the righteous will live by faith.
  • Chapter 2:6 to 20: the five woes against the oppressor and the declaration that the Lord is in his holy temple.
  • Chapter 3: Habakkuk's prayer, the glorious manifestation of God, and the final praise in the midst of scarcity.

Main themes

The first theme is the right to question. Habakkuk cries out how long, and the Lord does not rebuke him, but answers him. The Bible shows us that faith is not a forced silence: it is bringing our doubts and our pain to God. In the face of injustice, we can speak to him honestly. What matters is where we leave the question, whether we walk away from him or wait for his answer.

The second theme is living by faith. God tells him that the one who is proud is not upright in soul, but that the righteous will live by faith. Paul cites this phrase in Romans and Galatians to teach justification by faith in Christ. Trusting is not seeing everything clearly, it is resting on the character of God. Faith is waiting patiently, even if the vision seems to tarry.

The third theme is praise in trial. At the end, Habakkuk remembers God's past works and decides to rejoice even if the fig tree does not blossom and there is no fruit on the vines. His joy does not depend on circumstances, but on the Lord, who is his strength. It is a testimony of spiritual maturity: worshiping when everything is lacking and still feeling sustained.

Key verses

  • Habakkuk 1:2
  • Habakkuk 2:4
  • Habakkuk 2:14
  • Habakkuk 3:17-18

Reflecting on the verses

In Habakkuk 1:2, we hear the prophet's honest cry: how long shall I cry for help and you not listen. Habakkuk 2:4 is the center of the book, with the promise that the righteous will live by faith. In Habakkuk 2:14, it is announced that the earth will be filled with the knowledge of the glory of the Lord. And in Habakkuk 3:17-18, the prophet rejoices in God even if the harvest fails.

Application for today

Habakkuk encourages us to pray sincerely when we do not understand. We can write down our questions and wait patiently, remembering that God knows the end. He also teaches us to remember what God has done in the past to sustain our trust. And he challenges us to choose praise even in scarcity, because God is our strength today and always.

Questions for reflection

  • What hard question do I need to bring honestly to God today?
  • How can I trust in his plan when I do not understand it?
  • What reasons do I have to rejoice in the Lord even if other things are lacking?

Prayer

Lord, I bring you my questions and my fears. Increase my faith to live trusting in you when I do not understand. Even if everything fails, I want to rejoice in you, my strength. Amen.
