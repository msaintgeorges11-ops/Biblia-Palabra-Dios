🙏 Today’s Prayer

Lord God, Creator of heaven and earth, I thank you for this morning. When I look at the sky, the light of the sun, the clouds, and the birds, your wisdom amazes me. Everything you have made speaks of you, and today I want to listen with an attentive heart. Thank you that the same God who holds the stars also cares for me.

Father, sometimes my life fills with noise, tasks, and screens, and I forget to lift my eyes. Forgive me for thinking everything depends on me. Teach me to admire your work, to learn from it, and to give you the glory. Give me a curious mind, a humble heart, and the patience to seek the truth, knowing that you are the source of all wisdom.

Bless my work and my studies today. May what I do with my hands and my mind honor you and serve others. Watch over my family, and give me eyes to see your goodness in the little things. When the day ends, may I be able to say, like the psalmist, that your power is great and your understanding has no limit. In Jesus’ name, amen.

📚 Today’s Story

On November 15, 1630, the astronomer Johannes Kepler died in the city of Regensburg, in the Holy Roman Empire, at the age of 58. He had traveled there to claim money owed to him, fell ill a few days after arriving, and was buried in the Protestant cemetery of St. Peter, outside the city walls.

Kepler had been born on December 27, 1571, and was a man of Lutheran faith. As a young man he studied at Tübingen with the desire to become a pastor, but his life led him toward the stars. With Tycho Brahe’s careful records of the planet Mars, he discovered that the planets move in ellipses around the Sun, and published these findings in his work Astronomia Nova in 1609. In 1619, in his book Harmonices Mundi, he set out his third law of planetary motion.

For Kepler, studying the heavens was not an activity separate from his faith. He believed God had created the universe with order and that the human mind, made in the image of God, could grasp something of that order. For this reason he saw science as a way of admiring the Creator.

Kepler also had difficult moments with his church. Because he did not fully accept a doctrine about the Lord’s Supper, he was denied communion in Linz and was excluded in 1619. Even so, he wrote in favor of tolerance among Christians, saying that Christ the Lord was not, and is not, Lutheran, nor Calvinist, nor papist.

His life shows that faith and curiosity can walk hand in hand, and that the starry sky can lead us to worship the one who made it.

✝️ Today’s Message

Psalm 147 says something astonishing: God counts the number of the stars and calls them all by name. And right away it adds that his understanding is beyond measure. If the Lord knows and sustains every star, how could he not know your name, your worries today, and your needs? Kepler devoted his life to studying the order of the heavens and discovered that this order spoke of the wisdom of its Creator.

Psalm 148 calls on the sun, the moon, and the stars to praise the Lord, “for he commanded and they were created.” Creation is a silent hymn. We do not need to be scientists to hear it: we only need to stop and look. The problem is that we live with our heads down, caught up in the urgent. God has left us the sky, the light, and the night as daily reminders that he is in control and that we are far more valuable than we think.

There is also a lesson in humility in Kepler’s story. He spent years being wrong, redoing calculations, and correcting ideas, until he found the true shape of the orbits. Learning takes time and patience. Our faith grows in the same way: with doubts, corrections, and questions, but with a sincere desire to know God as he is.

For today, give yourself five minutes to look at the sky, even if only through a window. Give thanks for three things you have seen in creation. If you have tasks that require mental effort, begin them with a short prayer asking for wisdom. And when you feel small, remember that the Lord who calls the stars by name knows you too.

Go out today with your gaze lifted and your heart confident, because the God who orders the stars also orders your steps. May his peace guard your mind and his joy be your strength. Walk without fear: he goes before you.
