Theme: Our Father Who Art in Heaven

The Lord’s Prayer in the Sermon on the Mount

⏱ Approximate duration: 5 min

📖 Today’s Verses

Matthew 6:9-13
Psalm 16:7-9

🙏 Today’s Prayer

Our Father who art in heaven, hallowed be your name. As this day ends I come to you with gratitude, because you have sustained me every hour. Thank you for today’s bread, for health, for my family, and for all the small blessings I sometimes overlook. I acknowledge that you are a Father who is near and listens to me with love.

Forgive my debts as I also forgive my debtors. If someone offended me today, help me let go of resentment and rest without bitterness. If I failed in my words or my attitudes, cleanse me with your mercy. Deliver me from all evil and do not lead me into temptation. May your kingdom come to my home and your will be done in my life, on earth as it is in heaven.

I bless the Lord who counsels me; even at night my conscience instructs me. I keep you always before me, and therefore I will not be shaken. So my heart is glad and my soul rejoices; my body also will rest secure. Give me deep rest and wake me with renewed hope. Yours is the kingdom, the power, and the glory, forever. Thank you, Father, for your constant love. In Jesus’ name, amen.

📚 Today’s Story

In the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus taught his disciples to pray. Before giving them the prayer, he warned them not to pray like the hypocrites, who loved to do so on the street corners to be seen by others. He told them to go into their room, shut the door, and pray to the Father who is in secret, and that the Father, who sees in secret, would reward them.

Then he said to them: “Pray then like this: Our Father who art in heaven, hallowed be your name. Your kingdom come. Your will be done, on earth as it is in heaven.” With these first words, Jesus puts God at the center: his name, his kingdom, and his will. Before asking anything for ourselves, we learn to look to God as Father.

The prayer continues with simple and profound petitions: “Give us this day our daily bread. And forgive us our debts, as we also have forgiven our debtors. And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil.” It asks for daily sustenance, for forgiveness, and for protection. These are the needs we all have, in every age and in every place.

The most remarkable thing is the language: Jesus said “Our Father,” not “My Father.” He taught us to pray in community, remembering that we are part of a family. And right after the prayer, he emphasized the importance of forgiving others. This brief prayer, which we can recite in a few seconds, holds a whole life of trust, humility, and communion with God. Whoever prays it from the heart discovers that God delights in listening to his children, and that his answer is always one of love. That is why this prayer is still alive after so many centuries on the lips of believers in every language.

✝️ Today’s Message

There are nights when words run out. We are tired and do not know what to say. For those moments, Jesus left us a prayer we can repeat calmly. The Lord’s Prayer is not a magic formula, but a school of prayer where we learn to speak with God as children speak with their father.

You can use this prayer as a guide to close your day. Begin at the beginning: acknowledge God as Father and thank him for what you lived. Then ask for tomorrow’s bread, not for the whole week’s, learning to trust day by day. Next, examine your heart: is there someone you need to forgive? Say it quietly and hand it over to the Lord. Finally, ask for protection for the night and for what lies ahead.

A simple practice is to pray the Lord’s Prayer slowly, pausing on each phrase for a few seconds. At “give us this day our daily bread,” give thanks for what you had today. At “forgive us our debts,” honestly name what you want God to forgive you. At “deliver us from evil,” hand over your fears. You will see that such a brief prayer can fill the whole heart and bring you a deep calm.

The psalmist says that even at night his conscience instructs him, and that is why his heart is glad and his body rests secure. You, too, can end the day with that confidence. You do not have to have everything resolved, nor do your prayers have to be long. It is enough to come to him sincerely. The Father in heaven listens to you, knows you, and receives you with love every night.

May the Father who sees in secret give you peaceful sleep tonight and a heart free from resentment. Hand over to him what remains unfinished and rest in his care. His mercy will cover you through the night and welcome you with light at dawn.
