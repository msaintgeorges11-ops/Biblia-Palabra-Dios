📖 Book of Galatians

Free in Christ · 6 chapters

Context

Paul wrote this letter to the churches of Galatia, a region in central present-day Turkey, which he had visited himself on his missionary journeys. Shortly after he left, teachers arrived and claimed that Gentiles had to be circumcised and observe the law of Moses in order to be truly saved. Paul was alarmed, because he understood that this teaching endangered the very essence of the Gospel, and that is why he wrote with unusual intensity.

Central Message

The central message is that we are justified by faith in Jesus Christ and not by works of the law. Adding human requirements to grace amounts to abandoning the Gospel. Christ has set us free, and this freedom is not a license to sin, but the opportunity to serve one another in love and to walk guided by the Spirit. Galatians is the letter of Christian freedom and a warning against all legalism.

Structure of the Book

  • Chapters 1 to 2: the defense of the Gospel received by revelation and the encounter with Peter at Antioch.
  • Chapter 3: Abraham's faith, the role of the law and the promise fulfilled in Christ.
  • Chapter 4: from servants to sons, adoption and the illustration of Hagar and Sarah.
  • Chapter 5: freedom in Christ, the battle between the flesh and the Spirit and the fruit of the Spirit.
  • Chapter 6: restoring one who falls, bearing one another's burdens and the joy of the cross.

Main Themes

The first theme is grace versus legalism. Paul recalls that the law was not given to save, but to show sin and lead us to Christ. Now that faith has come, we are no longer under a guardian. To go back to depending on rules in order to gain God's acceptance is to return to a slavery from which Christ has delivered us.

The second theme is the believer's identity. Paul affirms that he has been crucified with Christ and that it is no longer he who lives, but Christ who lives in him. Those who believe are children of God, heirs according to the promise, and in Christ the barriers between Jew and Greek, slave and free, male and female disappear. All are one, and this unity is a powerful witness.

The third theme is life in the Spirit. Paul describes the battle between the desires of the flesh and those of the Spirit, and lists the fruit the Spirit produces: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness and self-control. Christian character is not manufactured by human effort, but it grows when we remain in fellowship with God and walk by the Spirit.

Key Verses

  • Galatians 2:20
  • Galatians 3:28
  • Galatians 5:1
  • Galatians 5:22-23

Reflecting on the Verses

In Galatians 2:20, Paul expresses the essence of the Christian life: Christ lives in us and loved us to the point of giving himself for us. In Galatians 3:28, the unity of all who are in Christ is proclaimed. In Galatians 5:1, we are urged to stand firm in the freedom Christ has given us and not to submit again to the yoke of slavery. And in Galatians 5:22-23, the fruit of the Spirit is described, the true seal of a transformed life.

Application for Today

Galatians invites us to check whether we have turned our faith into a list of requirements. Grace accepts us as we are, but the Spirit does not leave us as we are: he produces a new character. It also calls us to gently restore one who has fallen, remembering that we too may be tempted, and not to grow weary of doing good, for in due season we will reap.

Questions to Reflect On

  • Do I live my faith as a relationship of grace or as a list of requirements?
  • What fruit of the Spirit do I need to ask God to grow in me?
  • Whom can I help to get back on their feet with gentleness?

Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for setting me free. Keep me from all legalism, fill my life with the fruit of your Spirit and teach me to serve others with love. Amen.
