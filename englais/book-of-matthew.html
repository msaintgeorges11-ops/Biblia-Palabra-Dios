📖 Book of Matthew

The Promised King · 28 chapters

Context

Matthew, the former tax collector whom Jesus called to follow him, wrote this gospel with Jewish readers especially in mind. His purpose is clear: to demonstrate that Jesus is the Messiah announced by the prophets, the King of Israel and the Savior of the world. That is why he repeatedly says that something happened "to fulfill" what the prophet had said.

Central Message

The book gathers five great discourses of Jesus, including the Sermon on the Mount (chapters 5 to 7), and ends with the Great Commission: to go and make disciples of all nations. Matthew teaches us that following Jesus means living under his reign, seeking first his righteousness, and that he has promised to be with us always, to the end of the age.

Structure of the Book

  • Chapters 1 to 4: the birth of Jesus, the visit of the wise men, his baptism and the temptation in the wilderness.
  • Chapters 5 to 7: the Sermon on the Mount, with the Beatitudes and the Lord's Prayer.
  • Chapters 8 to 12: miracles, the calling of the twelve and the growing opposition of the religious leaders.
  • Chapters 13 to 20: the parables of the kingdom, Peter's confession and the teaching on life in community.
  • Chapters 21 to 28: the entry into Jerusalem, the cross, the resurrection and the Great Commission.

Main Themes

Matthew begins with a genealogy that connects Jesus to Abraham and David. This is not a mere list: it is a way of saying that the promises made to Israel are fulfilled in him. Throughout the book, the author stresses that many events took place so that what the prophets had said would be fulfilled, such as the birth in Bethlehem or the name Immanuel, which means God with us.

The book organizes Jesus' teaching into five great discourses: the Sermon on the Mount, the sending out of the twelve, the parables of the kingdom, life in community and the discourse on the last days. There we learn that the kingdom of heaven is not like the kingdoms of this world: it belongs to the poor in spirit, the meek and those who hunger for righteousness.

Matthew is also the gospel of the Church. It is the only one that mentions the word, and it gives instructions on forgiveness among brothers and on the restoration of one who falls. It ends with the Great Commission, which makes every believer a disciple who makes disciples.

Matthew also highlights the authority of Jesus. He teaches with authority and not like the scribes, heals the sick, calms the storm and forgives sins. Yet this authority does not crush, it invites: Jesus calls those who are weary and burdened to come to him and find rest, and he describes himself as gentle and lowly in heart. The King who rules is also the Shepherd who cares, and that is why his yoke is easy and his burden light.

Key Verses

  • Matthew 1:23
  • Matthew 5:3-10
  • Matthew 6:33
  • Matthew 28:18-20

Reflecting on the Verses

In Matthew 1:23, the name Immanuel sums up the message of the book: God did not stay far away, he came to be with us. In Matthew 5:3-10, the Beatitudes show the character of those who belong to Christ. In Matthew 6:33, Jesus teaches us to put God's kingdom first, trusting that the Father will take care of the rest. And in Matthew 28:18-20, the risen Lord declares that he has all authority and promises to be with his people every day.

Application for Today

Matthew invites us to review our priorities. What holds first place in our week: worries or the kingdom of God? He also reminds us that faith is lived out daily: forgiving, being gentle, helping the needy, praying in secret. And he gives us a concrete task: to share with others what we have received, knowing that Christ goes with us.

Questions to Reflect On

  • Which of the Beatitudes should I practice more this week?
  • Whom can I forgive, or with whom can I be reconciled?
  • With whom can I share today what Jesus has done in my life?

Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you that you are the promised King and God with us. Teach me to seek your kingdom first, to obey your Word and to share your love with those around me. Amen.
