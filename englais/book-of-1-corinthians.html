📖 Book of 1 Corinthians

A Church in Difficulty · 16 chapters

Context

Paul founded the church in Corinth on his second missionary journey and wrote this letter to it from Ephesus, around the year 55. Corinth was a wealthy, cosmopolitan city known for its immorality, and these influences had seeped into the Christian community. Some believers had sent questions to Paul, and others had informed him of divisions and serious sins. The letter answers both matters with pastoral clarity, firmness and much love.

Central Message

The central message is that the Church must live as the body of Christ: united, holy and moved by love. Paul corrects divisions, pride, immorality, lawsuits between brothers and disorder in worship, and reminds them that the Gospel of the cross changes the way we think and live. Chapter 13 shows that without love, spiritual gifts are worth nothing, and chapter 15 affirms that the resurrection of Christ is the foundation of our hope.

Structure of the Book

  • Chapters 1 to 4: the divisions in the Church and the wisdom of the cross versus the wisdom of the world.
  • Chapters 5 to 7: discipline in the face of sin, lawsuits between believers and teachings on marriage.
  • Chapters 8 to 10: Christian freedom, meat offered to idols and care for the weaker brother.
  • Chapters 11 to 14: order in worship, the Lord's Supper, spiritual gifts and the way of love.
  • Chapters 15 to 16: the resurrection of Christ and of believers, and the final instructions.

Main Themes

A striking theme is unity. Some said they belonged to Paul, others to Apollos, others to Cephas and others to Christ. Paul asks them whether Christ is divided and reminds them that leaders are servants of God, not party heads. The Church is one body made up of many members, and each is necessary. No gift is too small to be despised, nor too great to give rise to pride.

Another theme is holiness in the midst of a defiled culture. Paul urges them to flee immorality, because the believer's body is the temple of the Holy Spirit and was bought at a great price. He also teaches them to use freedom responsibly: not everything that is permitted is beneficial, and love leads us to give up what would cause others to stumble.

The third theme is the hope of the resurrection. In chapter 15, Paul recalls the Gospel he received: Christ died for our sins, was buried and rose on the third day. If Christ had not risen, faith would be in vain, but he has risen as the firstfruits of those who have died. That is why work done for the Lord is never in vain, and the believer can stand firm and unshaken.

Key Verses

  • 1 Corinthians 1:18
  • 1 Corinthians 6:19-20
  • 1 Corinthians 13:4-7
  • 1 Corinthians 15:58

Reflecting on the Verses

In 1 Corinthians 1:18, Paul explains that the word of the cross seems foolishness to some, but it is the power of God to those who are being saved. In 1 Corinthians 6:19-20, he reminds us that our body belongs to God and must glorify him. In 1 Corinthians 13:4-7, we find the description of the patient, kind and enduring love that must mark the whole Church. And in 1 Corinthians 15:58, the hope of the resurrection becomes an exhortation to stand firm and to abound in the work of the Lord.

Application for Today

This letter invites us to examine our attitudes within the Church. Do we seek to put ourselves forward or to serve? Do we defend our group or promote unity? It also calls us to watch over our witness in daily life and to measure our decisions not only by what is permitted to us, but by what builds up others. And above all, it reminds us that love is the most excellent way.

Questions to Reflect On

  • Is there a division or rivalry that I can help heal?
  • How can I honor God with my body and my decisions?
  • In which relationship do I need to show more patience and kindness?

Prayer

Lord, thank you for making me a member of your body. Remove pride and divisions from me, teach me to love with patience and help me to stand firm in the hope of the resurrection. Amen.
