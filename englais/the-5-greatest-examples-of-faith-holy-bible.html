📚 The 5 Greatest Examples of Faith - Holy Bible

Men and women of the Bible · Bible Word God

1. Abraham

He left his land without knowing where he was going, simply because God asked him to. "And he believed in the LORD; and he counted it to him for righteousness" (Genesis 15:6).

2. Noah

He built an ark when no one had ever seen such rain. He obeyed, and his household was saved (Hebrews 11:7).

3. Moses

He chose to be mistreated with the people of God rather than enjoy the pleasures of Egypt (Hebrews 11:24-26).

4. Rahab

A woman of Jericho who believed in the God of Israel and protected the spies. Her faith changed her story (Joshua 2; Hebrews 11:31).

5. David

He faced Goliath, saying: "I come to thee in the name of the LORD of hosts" (1 Samuel 17:45). His trust was in God, not in his own strength.

All of them failed in something, but they trusted God. That is the key: not a perfect faith, but a faith placed in a faithful God.

What we learn from Abraham

Abraham was seventy-five years old when God told him to leave his country and his family (Genesis 12:1-4). He did not know the destination, but he obeyed. Later, he waited years for the promised son. His faith was not perfect — there were moments of fear and mistakes — but God called him his friend (James 2:23).

The lesson: faith does not require understanding the whole road, only trusting the One who knows it. When God asks you to take a step, trust: he will give you the light for the next one.

What we learn from Noah

Noah lived in a time of great wickedness. God warned him of things not yet seen, and he built an ark for many years. He probably received mockery. Genesis 6:22 says: "Thus did Noah; according to all that God commanded him, so did he."

The lesson: sustained obedience is worth more than the approval of others. Sometimes following God means going against the current, and God takes care of those who obey him.

What we learn from Moses and Rahab

Moses grew up in Pharaoh's palace, but he chose to identify with the people of God. Afterward, he faced fear, the desert and the complaints of the people. His faith was formed over the years. He felt unable, but God told him: "Certainly I will be with thee" (Exodus 3:12).

Rahab was a woman with a difficult past and from an enemy nation, but she heard what God had done and believed. She was included in the lineage of Jesus (Matthew 1:5). The lesson: God uses whoever trusts in him, whatever their past.

What we learn from David

David was the youngest in his family, a shepherd. When everyone feared Goliath, he trusted that God would help him. Later, as king, he committed serious sins, and his prayer of repentance in Psalm 51 is a model of sincerity. God called him "a man after mine own heart" (Acts 13:22).

The lesson: faith includes returning to God when we fail. David did not hide his sin: he confessed it and received forgiveness.

Your own story of faith

These people encourage us, but God is also writing your story. Perhaps your "ark" is honest work in a difficult place, or raising your children in the faith, or forgiving someone who hurt you. Hebrews 12:1 reminds us that we are surrounded by a great cloud of witnesses.

Today, choose one of these examples and read his or her full story in the Bible. Ask yourself what step of faith God is asking you to take. Write it down, pray about it and do it with confidence.

Frequently asked questions

Are there other important examples? Yes: Joseph, Esther, Daniel, Deborah, Hannah, Paul and many more. Read Hebrews 11 in full, as well as the books of Genesis, Joshua, Esther and Daniel to discover their stories.

What is the greatest example? Jesus. Hebrews 12:2 commands us to fix our eyes on him, who endured the cross for the joy set before him.

To reflect on this week

Choose one person and read his or her full story. Ask yourself in what area of your life you can imitate their trust in God.

Suggested verses: Genesis 12:1-4, Genesis 6:9-22, Hebrews 11:24-27, Joshua 2:8-13 and 1 Samuel 17:45-47. Read one each day, ask yourself what it teaches you about God and what concrete step you can take. Finish with a short prayer, giving thanks for what you have learned and asking for help to put it into practice. Sharing what you discover with a loved one or a friend helps you remember it and may encourage others.

Prayer

Father, may my life also be an example of trust in you. Give me courage to obey you. Amen.
