📚 The Thief on the Cross and Paradise - Luke 23:43 - Holy Bible

A last-minute promise · Bible Word God

A man with no time left

Two criminals were crucified with Jesus. One of them insulted him. The other rebuked him: "Do you not fear God, since you are under the same sentence of condemnation?" (Luke 23:40). He acknowledged his guilt and his faith in Jesus' innocence.

A simple prayer

He said: "Remember me when you come into your kingdom." He had no works to present, nor time to be baptized or to serve. He had only faith and repentance.

Jesus' answer

Jesus answered him: "Truly, I say to you, today you will be with me in paradise" (v. 43). Salvation is by grace, not by merit. This thief reminds us that no one is too far from God.

If you feel it is already too late for you, this story is for you. Cry out to Jesus today: he listens.

The context of the crucifixion

Crucifixion was Rome's cruelest form of execution. Jesus was nailed between two criminals, fulfilling the prophecy of Isaiah 53:12: "he was numbered with the transgressors." The soldiers divided his garments, the leaders mocked, and the crowd watched. In the midst of that pain, Jesus prayed: "Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do" (Luke 23:34).

At first, according to Matthew and Mark, both criminals insulted him. But at some point, one of them changed. Something in Jesus' behavior touched his heart.

A sincere repentance

The thief did four things. He acknowledged God: "Do you not fear God?" He acknowledged his guilt: "We are receiving the due reward of our deeds." He acknowledged Jesus' innocence: "This man has done nothing wrong." And he placed his trust in him: "Remember me when you come into your kingdom."

These four attitudes sum up what it means to turn to God: a respectful fear, honesty about sin, faith in Christ and trust in his mercy.

What this teaches and what it does not

This story shows that salvation is a gift and that it is never too late as long as there is life. But it is no excuse for putting off the decision. No one knows how much time they have left. The Bible says: "Today, if you hear his voice, do not harden your hearts" (Hebrews 3:15).

Nor does it teach that baptism or works are unimportant for those who have the time. Whoever has been saved naturally wants to obey Christ. The thief could not do so because of circumstances, but his faith was real.

An open door for you

Perhaps you think you have done things that are too serious, or that you have been away for too long. The story of the thief is Jesus' answer to those thoughts. "I will never cast out whoever comes to me."

Pray now, without waiting for a special moment: "Jesus, remember me." Then begin to walk with him. Read his Word, look for a community and pray every day. That short prayer can be the beginning of a new life.

Frequently asked questions

Where does the comma go in Jesus' sentence? The original Greek had no punctuation. Almost all scholars place it before "today": "Truly, I say to you today, you will be with me." The promise holds for that very day, and the essential point is that he will be with Christ.

Was the thief baptized? There is no record of it. This shows us that the essential thing is faith in Christ, even though whoever is able to must obey everything he commanded.

To reflect on this week

Read the four Gospels and compare what they tell about the crucifixion. Each one adds details that enrich the story.

Suggested verses: Luke 23:32-43, Matthew 27:38-44, Isaiah 53:12, Romans 5:8 and Ephesians 2:8-9. Read one each day, ask yourself what it teaches you about God and what concrete step you can take. Finish with a short prayer, giving thanks for what you have learned and asking for help to put it into practice. Sharing what you discover with a loved one or a friend helps you remember it and may encourage others.
