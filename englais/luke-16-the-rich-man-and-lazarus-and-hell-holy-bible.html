📚 Luke 16 The Rich Man and Lazarus and Hell - Holy Bible

What this story of Jesus teaches · Bible Word God

The story

In Luke 16:19-31, Jesus tells the story of a rich man who lived in luxury and a beggar named Lazarus, covered with sores, lying at his gate. Both died. Lazarus was carried to Abraham's side; the rich man woke up in torment.

The lessons

First: after death there are consequences, and the present life matters. Second: Abraham tells the rich man that between them there is "a great chasm" and that no one can cross from one side to the other (v. 26). Third: wealth does not save, nor does poverty condemn; what matters is the heart and faith.

"Let them listen to Moses and the Prophets"

The rich man asked for someone to rise from the dead to warn his brothers. The answer: "If they do not hear Moses and the Prophets, neither will they be persuaded if someone should rise from the dead" (v. 31). God has already spoken to us in his Word.

Listening to it today is the finest gift you can give yourself, and also sharing it with your family.

The context of the story

Jesus tells this story to Pharisees who "loved money" and ridiculed him (Luke 16:14). His message was addressed to those who trusted in their position and wealth and despised the needy. That is why the details are so striking: the rich man was clothed in purple and fine linen and feasted sumptuously every day; Lazarus, whose name means "God helps," was covered with sores and longed for the crumbs.

Notice that the rich man is not condemned because he was rich, but because he lived without seeing Lazarus, who was at his gate. His sin was indifference and trusting in his possessions rather than in God.

Parable or true story?

Scholars debate whether this is a parable or an account of real events. It is the only story of Jesus in which a character is given a name, which some take as a sign that it is real. Others say it is a parable with elements from the culture of its time. In any case, what matters is the message Jesus wants to convey.

And that message is clear: the present life is an opportunity that does not return, and what we do with it has eternal consequences. There is no second chance after death.

What this teaches us about the Word

The rich man wanted an extraordinary sign to convince his brothers. But Abraham answers that Scripture is enough. God has already spoken. This should encourage us to open the Bible, rather than to seek strange experiences.

It is also a warning: a greater miracle does not change someone who does not want to listen. The same leaders who saw Lazarus of Bethany raised (John 11) wanted to put him to death (John 12:10). What changes the heart is the Word of God received with faith.

How to apply it

Look around you. Who is the Lazarus at your gate? It may be a lonely neighbor, a relative in need, a coworker who is suffering in silence. Jesus said: "As you did it to one of the least of these my brothers, you did it to me" (Matthew 25:40).

And examine where you place your trust. Paul writes to Timothy that the rich must not be haughty, nor "set their hopes on the uncertainty of riches, but on God, who richly provides us with everything to enjoy" (1 Timothy 6:17). Today is a good day to take a step: help someone and, above all, entrust your life to Jesus.

Frequently asked questions

Is it wrong to have money? No. The problem is to trust in money and forget your neighbor. Abraham himself was very rich, and so was Job. What matters is the attitude of the heart and the use we make of what God entrusts to us.

Can we pray for the dead based on this passage? The story says that there is a great chasm that prevents crossing from one side to the other; so the Bible invites us to decide in this life and to pray for the living, for their salvation and their needs.

To reflect on this week

Read the whole story aloud, Luke 16:19-31, and think of who needs your attention or help today.

Suggested verses: Luke 12:15, Proverbs 19:17, 1 Timothy 6:17-19, Matthew 25:35-40 and James 2:15-16. Read one each day, ask yourself what it teaches you about God and what concrete step you can take. Finish with a short prayer, giving thanks for what you have learned and asking for help to put it into practice. Sharing what you discover with a loved one or a friend helps you remember it and may encourage others.

Prayer

Father, open my eyes to the needs of those around me and my heart to your Word. May I not put it off until tomorrow. Amen.
