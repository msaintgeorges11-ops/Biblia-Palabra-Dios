📚 Does Hell Really Exist? What the Holy Bible Says

What does the Holy Bible say about its existence? · Bible Word God

The teaching of Jesus

Many people wonder whether hell is real or only a symbol. What is remarkable is that the one who spoke of it most was Jesus, the one who loved humanity most. In Mark 9:43-48, he speaks of Gehenna and of the seriousness of letting sin dominate us.

The rest of Scripture

Paul, Peter, Jude and John also warn of judgment. Revelation 20:15 says that anyone whose name was not found written in the book of life was thrown into the lake of fire. The Bible presents hell as a reality, not as a human invention.

A just and merciful God

God takes no pleasure in anyone's destruction: "Have I any pleasure at all that the wicked should die? saith the Lord GOD: and not that he should return from his ways, and live?" (Ezekiel 18:23). And Peter writes that God is patient, "not willing that any should perish, but that all should come to repentance" (2 Peter 3:9).

If you feel his call today, do not put it off. The door is still open: "Behold, now is the day of salvation" (2 Corinthians 6:2).

The most common objections

Many say: "A God of love would not send anyone to hell." This is an honest question. The biblical answer is that true love does not ignore justice. A judge who sets free someone guilty of serious crimes is not good, he is unjust. God is perfectly loving and perfectly just, and at the cross these two attributes meet: Christ bore the punishment so that the sinner could be forgiven.

Others think hell is just an idea invented to control people. But if that were so, why did Jesus, who devoted his life to healing, forgiving and serving, speak of it? He had no interest in manipulating. He spoke of it because he knew the reality and wanted no one to be lost.

What the Bible does not say

We should be careful. The Bible does not give us every detail. Christians from different traditions have debated for centuries the exact nature of the punishment: is it eternal in consciousness, or is it a final destruction? The important thing is neither to add what Scripture does not say nor to take away its seriousness. What is certain is that it is a grave and real consequence of rejecting God.

The Bible also does not authorize us to decide who is saved or who is lost. That decision belongs to God, who sees the heart. Our role is to proclaim the good news with love and to live it consistently.

God's answer to the problem

If hell is real, then the cross is all the more important. Jesus endured rejection and separation from God in our place. Peter says that Christ "hath once suffered for sins, the just for the unjust, that he might bring us to God" (1 Peter 3:18). The goal of it all is to bring us close to the Father.

Paul sums it up: "God commendeth his love toward us, in that, while we were yet sinners, Christ died for us" (Romans 5:8). God did not wait for us to be good. He took the first step.

A sincere invitation

If you have doubts, you do not need to pretend to certainty. You can tell God: "If you are real, show me the truth." The Bible promises: "Ye shall seek me, and find me, when ye shall search for me with all your heart" (Jeremiah 29:13).

Read the Gospel of John quietly. Talk with a believer you trust. And do not put the question off, because life is brief and the answer is too important.

Frequently asked questions

Can I know whether I will be saved? Yes. John wrote that whoever believes in the Son of God has eternal life (1 John 5:13). Assurance comes not from our feelings, but from God's promise.

What about those who have never heard the Gospel? The Bible says that the Judge of all the earth will do what is right (Genesis 18:25). We can trust in his justice and his mercy, while still feeling the urgency of sharing the message with others.

To reflect on this week

A serious subject calls for quiet reading and honest prayer. Do not rush to draw conclusions; let Scripture speak.

Suggested verses: John 3:36, Ezekiel 33:11, 2 Peter 3:9 and Romans 8:1. Read one each day, ask yourself what it teaches you about God and what concrete step you can take. Finish with a short prayer, giving thanks for what you have learned and asking for help to put it into practice. Sharing what you discover with a loved one or a friend helps you remember it and may encourage others.

Prayer
