📖 Book of Titus

A Faith Seen in Works · 3 chapters

Context

Paul wrote this letter to Titus, a faithful co-worker of Greek origin, a Gentile, who had accompanied him on several journeys. He had left him in Crete to set in order what remained to be done and to appoint elders in every town. The Cretans had a reputation for being liars and lazy, and among them were teachers who upset whole families with false doctrines. Paul writes to encourage Titus and remind him that sound doctrine must produce a transformed life.

Central Message

The central message is that the truth of the gospel is demonstrated by good works. Paul teaches that the grace of God has appeared, bringing salvation to all people, and trains us to renounce ungodliness and worldly passions, and to live self-controlled, upright and godly lives in the present age. We are not saved by works, but by God's mercy; yet we are saved to be a people zealous for good works, who in everything adorn the doctrine of God our Savior.

Structure of the Book

  • Chapter 1: the greeting, the appointment of elders of blameless character and the warning against false teachers.
  • Chapter 2: what fits sound doctrine for older men, older women, young people and servants.
  • Chapter 3: conduct toward authorities and others, the reminder of salvation by mercy and the final instructions.

Main Themes

The first theme is the importance of character in leadership. Paul asks that elders be blameless, the husband of one wife, with believing children, not arrogant, not quick-tempered, not greedy, and able to exhort with sound teaching. God does not entrust his Church to someone who only has skills, but to one who lives consistently with what he preaches. The example of the leaders sets the direction for the whole community.

The second theme is that doctrine and life go together. In chapter 2, Paul gives concrete instructions to each group in the Church: older men must be sober and dignified, older women must teach what is good, young people must be self-controlled and servants faithful in their work. All this has a reason: that the word of God may not be reviled and that the teaching may appear attractive to those who observe it.

The third theme is salvation by mercy. Paul recalls that we were once foolish, disobedient and slaves to passions, but when the goodness of God appeared, he saved us, not because of righteous things we had done, but according to his mercy, through the washing of regeneration and renewal by the Holy Spirit. This memory makes us humble and moves us to be kind toward everyone.

Key Verses

  • Titus 2:11-12
  • Titus 2:14
  • Titus 3:4-5
  • Titus 3:8

Reflecting on the Verses

In Titus 2:11-12, the grace of God appears as a teacher who trains us to say no to what dishonors and yes to a sober and godly life. In Titus 2:14, Paul recalls that Christ gave himself to redeem us and purify a people for his own possession. In Titus 3:4-5, we behold the goodness of God who saved us without our deserving it. And in Titus 3:8, the apostle insists that those who believe should devote themselves to good works, which are good and profitable for everyone.

Application for Today

Titus reminds us that our faith is seen in the small actions of each day: how we speak, how we work, how we treat authority and those who think differently. It is not about pretending, but about letting grace shape us. It also invites us to remember where God brought us from, so that we may treat with patience and gentleness those who are still lost.

Questions to Reflect On

  • What concrete good work can I do this week to bless others?
  • In what area does my conduct contradict what I say I believe?
  • How can I show patience toward someone who is difficult for me?

Prayer

God our Savior, thank you for your goodness and mercy. Teach me, by your grace, to live soberly, uprightly and godly, and make me zealous for good works. Amen.
