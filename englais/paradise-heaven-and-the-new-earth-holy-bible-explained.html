📚 Paradise, Heaven and the New Earth - Holy Bible Explained

How they are connected according to the Holy Bible · Bible Word God

Heaven, the presence of God

The Bible calls heaven the place where God dwells. Jesus said: "In my Father’s house are many mansions… I go to prepare a place for you" (John 14:2). Paul adds that our citizenship is in heaven (Philippians 3:20).

Paradise, being with Christ

When Jesus said to the criminal: "To day shalt thou be with me in paradise," he showed that believers are with the Lord when they leave this world. Paul also says that to depart is "to be with Christ; which is far better" (Philippians 1:23).

The new earth

God's final plan is to renew everything. "I saw a new heaven and a new earth" (Revelation 21:1), and Peter writes: "We, according to his promise, look for new heavens and a new earth, wherein dwelleth righteousness" (2 Peter 3:13). God himself will dwell with his people.

These are not three different promises, but stages of one and the same hope: being with God forever.

Why the confusion

Many people imagine that the believer's final destiny is to float on clouds forever. But the Bible speaks of something greater. It describes heaven as the presence of God, paradise as communion with Christ after death, and the new earth as the renewed creation where God will dwell with his people.

These ideas do not contradict each other. They are different aspects of one plan of salvation. While reading, it helps to ask which moment or which reality each passage refers to.

The intermediate state

Between the believer's death and the final resurrection, the Bible speaks of being with the Lord. Paul says that to leave this body is to be at home with the Lord (2 Corinthians 5:8). It is a state of comfort, but it is not the final goal. Believers await the resurrection of the body, and the Bible says that the Lord himself will descend from heaven and the dead in Christ will rise first (1 Thessalonians 4:16).

The Christian hope is not only that the soul goes to heaven, but that the whole human being is restored.

The new creation

God does not reject his creation; he renews it. Isaiah 65:17 speaks of "new heavens and a new earth." Romans 8:21 says that creation itself "shall be delivered from the bondage of corruption." And Revelation 21 shows the new Jerusalem coming down from heaven to earth. Heaven and earth are united when God dwells with his people.

This tells us that our work, our relationships and our care for creation are not useless. God has a future for everything he has made.

What to do with this hope

First, to rest. If you have lost a loved one who trusted in Christ, you can be confident that he or she is with him. Second, to live with purpose. Paul ends his great chapter on the resurrection by saying: "Be ye stedfast, unmoveable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, forasmuch as ye know that your labour is not in vain in the Lord" (1 Corinthians 15:58).

Third, to share it. Most people live without hope. A kind word, a prayer or an invitation to church can make the difference. Today, take a few minutes to thank God for the future he has prepared.

Frequently asked questions

What is the most important difference? All these terms point to the same thing: living with God. It is not necessary to resolve every detail in order to trust Christ and wait with joy.

What if another Christian thinks differently? There are subjects on which sincere believers do not agree. Speak with respect, return to Scripture and seek unity on the essentials: Christ crucified and risen.

To reflect on this week

Read these passages in order, noting what each one says about our destiny and the presence of God.

Suggested verses: John 14:1-3, Philippians 3:20-21, 1 Thessalonians 4:13-18, Isaiah 65:17 and Revelation 21:1-4. Read one each day, ask yourself what it teaches you about God and what concrete step you can take. Finish with a short prayer, giving thanks for what you have learned and asking for help to put it into practice. Sharing what you discover with a loved one or a friend helps you remember it and may encourage others.

Prayer
