Theme: Commit Your Way

The Landing at Brixham in 1688

⏱ Approximate duration: 5 min

📖 Today’s Verses

Psalm 37:5-7
Psalm 33:18-20

🙏 Today’s Prayer

God of all hope, thank you for letting me wake today. When I open my eyes and think about what awaits me, I want to remember that my times are in your hands. You know the road I must travel, and so I come to you simply, laying at your feet my plans, my doubts, and my fears. Thank you for watching over me with eyes of love.

Lord, teach me to commit my way to you and to trust that you will act. Sometimes I want to get ahead, control everything, and see results at once. Give me patience to wait on you and calm to rest when things do not turn out as I expected. May I not be troubled by those who seem to prosper while I wait, but keep my gaze fixed on your faithfulness.

I pray for my family, for my work, and for all who need your help today. Free us from all fear, sustain us in need, and let our hope be placed in your mercy. May our soul wait for you, for you are our help and our shield. Be with me in every decision of this day and may my steps glorify you. Thank you, Father, for never leaving me alone. In Jesus’ name, amen.

📚 Today’s Story

On November 5, 1688 (by the Julian calendar then used by the English), William of Orange, Prince of the Netherlands, landed at Brixham, on the coast of Devon, England, at the head of a large fleet. He came at the invitation of a group of English leaders concerned about the political and religious direction of the kingdom. This landing was the beginning of what history remembers as the Glorious Revolution.

According to tradition, his banner bore a motto that expressed his purpose: to maintain the liberties of England and the Protestant religion. A few weeks later, King James II left the throne, and the following year William and his wife Mary were proclaimed monarchs. In 1689 the Toleration Act was passed, which granted many dissenting Protestants the freedom to gather for worship.

Beyond the political details, which historians still study, this event reminds us of what thousands of believers of that era felt: that God guided events in his own time. They had lived years of tension and uncertainty, and many prayed that their families might worship freely and read the Bible without fear. Seeing a fleet arrive on the coast was, for them, an answer to long waiting.

Scripture teaches us that same lesson: “Commit your way to the Lord; trust in him, and he will act.” It does not promise that it will be immediate or easy, but that God works. Whoever waits on him is not disappointed, for the eyes of the Lord are on those who fear him, on those who hope in his steadfast love, to deliver their soul from death and keep them alive in famine. For us, it is an invitation to wait with serenity and to give thanks for the freedom to worship.

✝️ Today’s Message

Waiting is one of the hardest things in life. We want quick answers, immediate solutions, and roads without obstacles. But the history of faith shows us that many times God works in silence, preparing things we cannot yet see. The believers who lived before November 5, 1688, waited years for change, and they learned that God is never late.

Psalm 37 gives us three verbs for today: commit, trust, and rest. To commit is to place our way in God’s hands, like handing a load to someone stronger. To trust is to believe that he will fulfill his promise even when we cannot see how. To rest is to be at ease in that certainty, without fretting over the success of others or the slowness of our plans.

Put this into practice right now. Take a minute and write down a request you have carried in your heart for a long time. Then say aloud: “Lord, I commit it to you.” During the day, every time the worry returns, repeat that phrase as a small prayer. You do not need to control the outcome; you only need to take the step within your reach today, with honesty and diligence.

And remember that God is watching over you. The psalmist says that his eyes are on those who hope in his steadfast love. You are not a number in the course of history, but someone he cares for. Perhaps today you do not see the fleet arriving on the coast, but you can be sure that the one who sustains you is still at work. Wait in peace, work with care, and pray with confidence, for our soul waits for the Lord; he is our help and our shield.

Go out with a calm heart, knowing that your steps are guarded by the Lord. Wait on him with patience and do not run ahead of the time of his grace. May his peace cover you from this morning until evening falls.
