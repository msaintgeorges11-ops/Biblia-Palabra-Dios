📚 What Is Paradise According to the Holy Bible

What the Holy Bible teaches · Bible Word God

A garden from the beginning

The word paradise evokes a garden. In Genesis 2, God planted Eden and placed man there to live in communion with him. There was neither pain nor death there. Sin broke this harmony, but God never stopped seeking humanity.

Paradise in the New Testament

The Bible uses this word three times: Jesus to the criminal on the cross (Luke 23:43), Paul when he speaks of having been caught up into paradise (2 Corinthians 12:4) and Revelation 2:7: "To him that overcometh will I give to eat of the tree of life, which is in the midst of the paradise of God."

A certain hope

Paradise is being with God. It is rest for the weary, comfort for those who have wept, and life forever. It does not depend on our merits, but on the work of Christ: "Father, I will that they also, whom thou hast given me, be with me where I am" (John 17:24).

The origin of the word

The word "paradise" comes from an ancient term meaning an enclosed garden or park. The Greek translation of the Old Testament used it for the garden of Eden. That is why, when Jesus speaks of paradise, he evokes a place of full life, beauty and communion with God, like the one Adam and Eve lost.

What is beautiful is that God's plan was not to abandon humanity, but to give us back what we had lost, and even more. The Bible begins with a garden and ends with a city where the tree of life grows (Revelation 22:1-2).

What we know about paradise

The Bible does not describe everything, but it tells us what is essential. It is a place where Christ is. Paul says he desires "to depart, and to be with Christ; which is far better" (Philippians 1:23). It is a place of rest and comfort: in the story of the rich man and Lazarus, Lazarus is comforted (Luke 16:25). And it is a place of joy: "In thy presence is fulness of joy; at thy right hand there are pleasures for evermore" (Psalm 16:11).

We also know what will no longer be there: no tears, no pain, no death. This promise fills with hope those who have lost a believing loved one.

Who it is for

Paradise is for those who put their trust in Christ. It is not a reward for the perfect, but a gift for the forgiven. The criminal on the cross had nothing to offer; he received the promise because he believed. This reminds us that salvation is by grace.

But this faith bears fruit. Whoever truly trusts in Jesus begins to love what he loves, to forgive and to serve. Paul puts it this way: "If any man be in Christ, he is a new creature: old things are passed away; behold, all things are become new" (2 Corinthians 5:17).

Living today with hope

Thinking about paradise does not take us away from the present life, but gives us the strength to live it. Whoever knows where he is going can endure the road. Paul encourages: "For our light affliction, which is but for a moment, worketh for us a far more exceeding and eternal weight of glory" (2 Corinthians 4:17).

Today, in the midst of your trials, lift your eyes. Talk to God about your fears. And share this hope with someone who needs it, for paradise is a gift that grows when it is shared.

Frequently asked questions

Will we recognize our loved ones? The Bible does not give many details, but the hope of reunion appears in 1 Thessalonians 4:13-18, where Paul comforts those who mourn their dead in Christ.

Is paradise a physical place? The Bible speaks of a new earth and resurrected bodies, not of a vague state. It will be a real life, with God, full and without evil.

To reflect on this week

Meditate on the hope God has prepared. This hope changes the way we face today's difficulties.

Suggested verses: Luke 23:43, 2 Corinthians 12:2-4, Revelation 2:7, Philippians 1:21-23 and Psalm 16:11. Read one each day, ask yourself what it teaches you about God and what concrete step you can take. Finish with a short prayer, giving thanks for what you have learned and asking for help to put it into practice. Sharing what you discover with a loved one or a friend helps you remember it and may encourage others.

Prayer

Father, thank you for the hope of paradise. Help me to live today knowing that you are waiting for me. In Jesus' name, amen.
