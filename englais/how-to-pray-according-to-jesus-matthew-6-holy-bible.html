📚 How to Pray According to Jesus (Matthew 6) - Holy Bible

The model Jesus taught us · Bible Word God

Pray in secret

Jesus taught: "But thou, when thou prayest, enter into thy closet, and when thou hast shut thy door, pray to thy Father which is in secret; and thy Father which seeth in secret shall reward thee openly" (Matthew 6:6). Prayer is not made to impress, but to be with God.

Without vain repetition

God does not need many words. "For your Father knoweth what things ye have need of, before ye ask him" (v. 8). Speak simply, like a child to his father.

The Lord's Prayer

Jesus gave a model (vv. 9-13): honor his name, ask for his kingdom to come, ask for daily bread, for forgiveness and for protection from evil.

You can pray in your own words following this order: worship, ask that his will be done, present your needs, forgive and trust.

Before the Lord's Prayer

Jesus begins by warning against prayer done to be seen. The hypocrites, he says, prayed on the street corners to be seen by people, and that was their reward. Prayer is not a performance; it is a conversation with the Father. That is why he insists on the secret place: a corner of the house, a moment of silence, a walk.

He also warns against meaningless repetition, as the pagans did. It is not that repeating is wrong, but God does not hear us by the number of our words. He listens to the heart.

Our Father in heaven

Jesus teaches us to begin by calling God "Father." This is an immense privilege: the Creator of the universe lets us approach him as his children. Then, "our": we do not pray alone, but as members of a family. And "in heaven": he is powerful and above all things.

"Hallowed be thy name" means that we want to honor God. "Thy kingdom come" is a request for his rule of justice and peace to spread. "Thy will be done" is about submitting our plans to his.

Bread, forgiveness and protection

"Give us this day our daily bread" teaches us to depend on God for the everyday. We do not ask for years ahead, but for today. "And forgive us our debts, as we forgive our debtors" joins the forgiveness we receive and the forgiveness we give. Jesus underlines this right afterward: if we forgive others, the Father will forgive us (6:14).

"And lead us not into temptation, but deliver us from evil" acknowledges our weakness and asks for God's protection. Many ancient versions add: "For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever. Amen."

How to practice it every day

You can use this model as a guide. Begin with worship: thank God for who he is. Ask that his will be done in your life. Present your needs for the day. Confess what you need to confess and forgive those who have wronged you. Finish by asking for his protection and entrusting yourself to him.

Do it consistently, even if only for five minutes. With time, prayer will stop being an obligation and become your refuge. Jesus said: "Ask, and it shall be given you; seek, and ye shall find; knock, and it shall be opened unto you" (Matthew 7:7).

Frequently asked questions

Should I recite the Lord's Prayer? Many churches pray it together, and it is beautiful. Jesus gave it as a model and as a prayer; use it as you wish, but without turning it into an empty routine.

What if I do not know what to say? Start with gratitude, then say what is in your heart. Romans 8:26 says that the Spirit helps us in our weakness and prays for us.

To reflect on this week

This week, pray the Lord's Prayer phrase by phrase, pausing on each one to talk with God about it.

Suggested verses: Matthew 6:5-15, Luke 11:1-4, Philippians 4:6-7, 1 Thessalonians 5:16-18 and Matthew 7:7-11. Read one each day, ask yourself what it teaches you about God and what concrete step you can take. Finish with a short prayer, giving thanks for what you have learned and asking for help to put it into practice. Sharing what you discover with a loved one or a friend helps you remember it and may encourage others.
