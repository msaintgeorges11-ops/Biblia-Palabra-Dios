📖 Song of Solomon

Faithful and pure love · 8 chapters

Background

The book, also called the Song of Songs, is attributed to Solomon, although its title can also be understood as a song dedicated to him. It was written in the 10th century BC, in the time of Israel's splendor. It is a collection of love poems between a bridegroom and his bride, with the participation of a chorus of the women of Jerusalem, and it was received by God's people as inspired Scripture.

Central message

The central message is that married love is a gift of God, good and beautiful, to be lived with faithfulness, self-giving, and purity. The couple admire one another, seek one another, and belong to one another. The book celebrates desire within the covenant of marriage and warns that love must not be awakened before its time. Many believers also see in it a reflection of Christ's love for his Church.

Structure of the book

  • Chapters 1 and 2: the meeting of the lovers, the mutual praise, and the invitation of spring.
  • Chapters 3 and 4: the beloved's nighttime search, the wedding procession, and the beauty of the bride at the wedding.
  • Chapters 5 and 6: the absence of the beloved, the bride's anguish, and her praise of the beloved before the daughters of Jerusalem.
  • Chapter 7: the bridegroom's renewed admiration and the bride's response of surrender.
  • Chapter 8: the seal of love as strong as death and the close of the poem with the bride's invitation.

Main themes

The first theme is married love as a gift from God. The book does not hide physical attraction, but presents it within the framework of commitment. The words of esteem are tender and delicate. In the face of a culture that trivializes intimacy, this poem reminds us that the body and affection, rightly guided, glorify the Creator and strengthen the home.

The second theme is faithfulness and the care of love. The bride compares herself to a locked garden, and it is repeated that love must not be awakened until it so desires. Difficulties appear too: absence, distance, and reproaches. True love is cultivated through communication, patience, and reconciliation, and is defended against whatever could harm it, like the little foxes that ruin the vineyards.

The third theme is a love that reflects Christ. Since ancient times, interpreters have seen in the bridegroom an image of the Lord who loves his people with ardor and faithfulness. The purest human love points to another, greater one. A love as strong as death reminds us of the One who gave his life for his Church and calls us to respond to him.

Key verses

  • Song of Solomon 2:4
  • Song of Solomon 2:16
  • Song of Solomon 8:6
  • Song of Solomon 8:7

Reflecting on the verses

In 2:4, the bride remembers that her beloved brought her to the banqueting house and that his banner over her is love. In 2:16, she declares their mutual belonging: he is hers and she is his. In 8:6, she asks to be set as a seal upon his heart and upon his arm, for love is as strong as death. And in 8:7, she affirms that many waters cannot quench love, nor floods drown it.

Application for today

Married couples can renew their love through words of esteem, time spent together, and a renewed commitment. Singles can learn to wait and to guard their hearts, without haste or pressure. We can all remember that human love is an imperfect reflection of God's love, which sought us first and never fails and never runs dry.

Questions for reflection

  • What concrete gestures can strengthen love in my marriage or my family today?
  • How can I guard my heart and my purity while I wait?
  • What does this book teach me about Christ's love for his Church?

Prayer

Lord, thank you for the gift of love. Teach me to love with faithfulness, tenderness, and purity. Protect the homes of your people, and may my heart always respond to the love with which you first loved me. Amen.
