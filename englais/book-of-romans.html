📖 Book of Romans

Justified by Faith · 16 chapters

Context

Paul wrote this letter to the believers in Rome, probably from Corinth, around the year 57. He had not yet visited that city, but he wanted to do so and wished to present to them clearly the gospel he preached. The church in Rome was made up of Jews and Gentiles, and tensions arose between them over the law, customs and the worth of each group. Romans is his most orderly exposition of the Christian faith and has been decisive in the history of the Church, from Augustine to Luther and Wesley.

Central Message

Paul shows that all, Jews and Gentiles, have sinned and fall short of the glory of God, but are justified freely by his grace, through the redemption that is in Christ Jesus. This justification is received by faith, and not by works. Nothing can separate us from the love of God, and that is why we respond by presenting our bodies as a living sacrifice and by renewing our minds. Romans gives us assurance: there is no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus.

Structure of the Book

  • Chapters 1 to 3: the condition of all humanity under sin and the need for a Savior.
  • Chapters 4 to 5: justification by faith, the example of Abraham and peace with God.
  • Chapters 6 to 8: the new life, the struggle against sin and victory through the Spirit.
  • Chapters 9 to 11: Israel's place in God's plan and his mercy toward all.
  • Chapters 12 to 16: the believer's practical life, love, submission to authorities and unity in the Church.

Main Themes

The first great theme of Romans is grace. Paul insists that no one can earn God's favor, for we all need forgiveness. Abraham was counted righteous because he believed God, before any law or ritual. This truth frees the believer from the burden of trying to deserve what can only be received as a gift, and leads him to live in gratitude rather than in fear.

The second theme is life in the Spirit. In chapters 6 to 8, Paul describes the inner struggle of the believer, who desires to do good and sometimes stumbles. The answer is not despair, but the work of the Spirit who makes us children of God, helps us in our weakness and intercedes for us. Chapter 8 culminates in the certainty that God works all things together for the good of those who love him.

The third theme is practical unity. After explaining the doctrine, Paul asks for sincere love, hospitality, humility and respect toward those who think differently on secondary matters. The strong must support the weak, and no one should despise his brother. In this way, the theology of Romans does not remain in the realm of ideas, but becomes a concrete way of living together.

Key Verses

  • Romans 1:16-17
  • Romans 3:23-24
  • Romans 8:1
  • Romans 12:1-2

Reflecting on the Verses

In Romans 1:16-17, Paul declares that he is not ashamed of the gospel, for it is the power of God for the salvation of everyone who believes. In Romans 3:23-24, he sums up man's need and the divine provision: all have sinned, but we are justified by his grace. In Romans 8:1, we find rest, because there is no condemnation for those who are in Christ. And in Romans 12:1-2, Paul invites us to respond with an offered life and a renewed mind.

Application for Today

Romans invites us to rest in grace without ceasing to obey. Whoever feels condemned can return to Christ with confidence, and whoever feels proud can remember that everything is a gift. It also encourages us to take care of our mind: what we read, hear and think shapes our life. Finally, it calls us to welcome with love those who are different, knowing that Christ has welcomed us.

Questions to Reflect On

  • Am I trying to earn God's favor or am I resting in his grace?
  • What area of my mind needs to be renewed by the Word?
  • Whom do I need to welcome with more patience and respect?

Prayer

Father, thank you for justifying me by faith in Jesus Christ. Renew my mind, strengthen my hope and teach me to love others as you have loved me. Amen.
