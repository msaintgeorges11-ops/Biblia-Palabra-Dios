🌅 What Does the Bible Say About Depression and Deep Sadness?

Hope and comfort when the soul is downcast

You are not alone in this darkness

Depression and deep sadness affect many people, including those who believe in God. The Bible does not hide these experiences. It shows men and women of faith who felt exhausted, downcast and without any desire to live. And it also shows how God sustained them. If you feel this way today, these words are for you, with much tenderness.

Biblical examples of despondency

Elijah, after a great victory, fled in fear, sat down under a tree and asked to die (1 Kings 19:3-4). God did not rebuke him. He sent him rest, food and an angel who touched him tenderly (1 Kings 19:5-8). Job lost his children, his possessions and his health, and expressed his pain in very raw terms (Job 3:1-3). Jeremiah, called the weeping prophet, lamented his situation (Lamentations 3:19-20).

David wrote many psalms out of pain: “Why are you downcast, O my soul? And why are you disquieted within me? Hope in God” (Psalm 42:5). Notice that he talks to himself, acknowledges his sadness and decides to hope in God. The Bible does not demand that we pretend to be cheerful.

God close to the brokenhearted

The psalmist affirms that the Lord is near to the brokenhearted and saves those who are crushed in spirit (Psalm 34:18). He also says that God heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds (Psalm 147:3). Jesus himself wept at the tomb of his friend Lazarus (John 11:35). And in Gethsemane he said that his soul was sorrowful, even to death (Matthew 26:38). Our Lord understands pain.

A message about faith and emotional health

Depression is neither a sin nor a lack of faith. It can have biological, emotional and circumstantial causes. Just as someone who suffers from a physical illness consults a doctor, someone who suffers from depression can and should seek professional help. God works through prayer, but also through medicine, therapy and the support of people who love us. Paul recommended to Timothy a little wine for the sake of his stomach and his frequent ailments (1 Timothy 5:23), which shows that remedies were not incompatible with faith.

Steps that can help you

Talk to someone. Reach out to a family member, a pastor or a friend. Sharing lightens the burden (Galatians 6:2).

Ask for professional help. A doctor or a psychologist can guide you.

Take care of the basics. Rest, eat well, go out for a walk and look for sunlight.

Pray honestly. Tell God exactly what you feel. The Psalms are a good model.

Move forward in small steps. Just one step a day is already moving forward.

A promise for difficult days

Paul writes that the God of all comfort comforts us in all our afflictions (2 Corinthians 1:3-4). And the psalmist says that weeping may stay for the night, but joy comes in the morning (Psalm 30:5). The night may be long, but it is not eternal.

If you have thoughts of harming yourself

If at any point you think about ending your life, seek help immediately. Call emergency services or a helpline in your country, and tell a trusted person right away. Your life has value to God and to many people, even if today you do not feel that way. You do not have to go through this alone.

A prayer of comfort

Lord, you see my weariness and my sadness. I have no strength, but I come to you as I am. Take my heart, heal my wounds and send me people who will help me. Give me hope to take the next step. Thank you for being close to me. In Jesus’ name, amen.
