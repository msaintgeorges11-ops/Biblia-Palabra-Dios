📖 Book of Acts

The Church on the Move · 28 chapters

Context

The book of Acts was written by Luke, the beloved physician, as a sequel to his Gospel. If the Gospel tells what Jesus began to do and to teach, Acts recounts what Jesus continued to do by his Spirit in the Church. It covers about thirty years, from the Lord's ascension to Paul's imprisonment in Rome, and shows how a small group of fearful disciples became a community that carried the message from Jerusalem to the ends of the earth.

Central Message

The central message is that the Holy Spirit gives believers the power to be witnesses of Christ. Jesus told his disciples that they would receive power and would be his witnesses in Jerusalem, in all Judea, in Samaria and to the ends of the earth, and the book follows exactly that path. Acts teaches us that the mission depends neither on human strength, nor on resources, nor on favorable circumstances, but on God working through people willing to obey him, even in the midst of persecution.

Structure of the Book

  • Chapters 1 to 2: the ascension of Jesus, the waiting in prayer and the coming of the Holy Spirit at Pentecost.
  • Chapters 3 to 7: the first years of the Church in Jerusalem, the miracles, the opposition and the testimony of Stephen.
  • Chapters 8 to 12: the Gospel reaches Samaria, the conversion of Saul, the baptism of Cornelius and the opening to the Gentiles.
  • Chapters 13 to 20: Paul's missionary journeys, the council of Jerusalem and the founding of churches in many cities.
  • Chapters 21 to 28: Paul's arrest, his defenses before the authorities, the shipwreck and his arrival in Rome.

Main Themes

Acts presents a Church that lives by prayer and fellowship. The first believers devoted themselves to the apostles' teaching, the breaking of bread, prayer and sharing what they had. It was not a perfect Church, for there are conflicts, mistakes and disagreements in it, but it was a community where God was at work and where each member felt responsible for the others.

Another constant theme is courage in the face of opposition. Peter and John are brought before the Sanhedrin and declare that they cannot help speaking about what they have seen and heard. Stephen dies proclaiming Christ, and Paul endures prisons, floggings and shipwrecks without abandoning his calling. The book shows that persecution, far from stopping the Gospel, spreads it with greater force.

Finally, Acts shows that the Gospel is for everyone. Philip talks with an Ethiopian, Peter enters the house of the centurion Cornelius and Paul preaches to Jews and Greeks without distinction. God tears down the barriers of race, culture and social position to form one people. This vision remains a challenge for every Christian community that wishes to resemble the Church of the first century.

Key Verses

  • Acts 1:8
  • Acts 2:42-47
  • Acts 4:12
  • Acts 16:31

Reflecting on the Verses

In Acts 1:8, Jesus promises the power to be witnesses, and this promise defines the mission of the Church in every age. In Acts 2:42-47, we see the portrait of a community that learns, prays and shares with joy. In Acts 4:12, Peter declares clearly that salvation is found in Christ alone. And in Acts 16:31, Paul's answer to the jailer at Philippi sums up the Gospel: believe in the Lord Jesus Christ, and you will be saved, you and your household.

Application for Today

Acts challenges us not to wait for ideal conditions to speak of Christ. The first disciples prayed, waited for the Spirit's direction, then obeyed. Today too, we can begin with what is close at hand: our family, our work, our neighborhood. In addition, the book invites us to value fellowship: a faith lived in solitude grows weak, but a faith shared is strengthened and multiplied.

Questions to Reflect On

  • Where is God calling me to be a witness of Christ this week?
  • What barrier keeps me from approaching people different from me?
  • What place do prayer and fellowship have in my daily life?

Prayer

Lord, thank you for your Spirit who gives power to your Church. Give me courage to speak of you, love to welcome everyone and faithfulness to follow your call to the end. Amen.
