📖 Book of Hebrews

Christ Is Better · 13 chapters

Context

The epistle to the Hebrews was written to believers of Jewish background who were under pressure and persecution, and were tempted to return to Judaism to avoid suffering. The author is unknown: some attribute it to Paul, others to Barnabas, to Apollos or to another co-worker. What is certain is his mastery of the Old Testament and his skill in showing that all the shadows of the old law pointed to Jesus Christ. It is a letter that combines deep exposition with very practical exhortations.

Central Message

The central message is that Christ is superior to everything: to the prophets, to the angels, to Moses, to the Levitical priesthood and to the old sacrifices. Jesus is the Son of God, the great high priest who offered himself once for all to take away sin. That is why believers must not turn back, but draw near with confidence to the throne of grace, hold fast the confession of their hope and run with perseverance the race set before them, looking to Jesus.

Structure of the Book

  • Chapters 1 to 2: the Son is superior to the angels and made himself like us in order to save us.
  • Chapters 3 to 4: Christ is superior to Moses and Joshua, and God's rest remains available.
  • Chapters 5 to 7: Christ's high priesthood in the order of Melchizedek.
  • Chapters 8 to 10: the new covenant, the heavenly sanctuary and the single, sufficient sacrifice of Jesus.
  • Chapters 11 to 13: the heroes of faith, the Lord's discipline and the exhortations for living out the faith.

Main Themes

The first theme is the superiority of Christ. The author begins by affirming that God, who had spoken long ago by the prophets, has spoken to us in these last days by his Son, the radiance of his glory and sustainer of all things. Compared with the old institutions, Jesus is the mediator of a better covenant, founded on better promises. Nothing and no one can replace him, and that is why going back would mean losing what is best.

The second theme is persevering faith. Chapter 11 presents a list of men and women who trusted God even without seeing the fulfillment of the promises: Abel, Enoch, Noah, Abraham, Sarah, Moses, Rahab and many others. They form a great cloud of witnesses who encourage us to throw off every burden and to run with perseverance. Faith is not vague optimism, but the assurance of things hoped for and the conviction of things not seen.

The third theme is access to God. Since Jesus is our high priest, who was tempted in every way as we are, yet without sin, we can draw near to the throne of grace to receive mercy and find grace to help in time of need. This frees us from fear and encourages us not to give up meeting together, but to exhort one another, all the more as we see the Day drawing near.

Key Verses

  • Hebrews 4:15-16
  • Hebrews 10:23-25
  • Hebrews 11:1
  • Hebrews 12:1-2

Reflecting on the Verses

In Hebrews 4:15-16, we see a high priest who understands our weaknesses and invites us to draw near with confidence. In Hebrews 10:23-25, we are urged to hold fast to our hope and to stir one another up to love and good works. In Hebrews 11:1, faith is defined as the assurance of things hoped for. And in Hebrews 12:1-2, the Christian race is presented with eyes fixed on Jesus, who is the founder and perfecter of faith.

Application for Today

Hebrews encourages us to persevere when weariness or pressure tempt us to give up. We can remember that Jesus has already walked the road of suffering and that he continues to intercede for us. It also invites us to value community: faith is better sustained when we walk with others. And it reminds us that God's discipline, though painful, is a sign of love and produces a fruit of peace.

Questions to Reflect On

  • What burden do I need to lay down in order to run the race of faith better?
  • Am I drawing near with confidence to the throne of grace?
  • How can I encourage a brother who is on the verge of giving up?

Prayer

Lord Jesus, thank you for being my faithful high priest. Give me faith to persevere, confidence to draw near to you and eyes fixed on you until the end of the race. Amen.
