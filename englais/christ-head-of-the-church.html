Theme: Christ, Head of the Church

The Act of Supremacy of 1534

⏱ Approximate duration: 6 min

📖 Today’s Verses

Matthew 7:24-27
Psalm 46:1-3

🙏 Today’s Prayer

Lord God, I thank you for this sunrise and for the life you grant me. Before the day claims me with its tasks, I want to stop in your presence and acknowledge that you are the foundation of all that I am. Thank you for your Word, which is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path, and for your Son Jesus Christ, the solid rock on which I can stand.

I ask that today I build my life on your teachings. May I not be content merely to hear them, but put them into practice in my home, my work, and my relationships. When the rains, the floods, and the winds of trial come, may I not be afraid, because my house is founded on you. Be my refuge and my strength, a very present help in trouble.

Thank you that your Church has only one head, who is Christ. Bless all who serve you in every place, the pastors, the teachers, and the simple believers who pray in silence. Give me humility not to put my trust in men, but in you. Guide me with your Spirit, give me wisdom and peace, and help me reflect your love throughout this day. In Jesus’ name, amen.

📚 Today’s Story

On November 3, 1534, the English Parliament passed the Act of Supremacy. It declared King Henry VIII the supreme head of the Church of England, thereby breaking the authority the pope had until then exercised over the Church in that kingdom. It is a well-documented fact that marked a deep turning point in the religious history of England.

That act had great consequences. It changed the way the Church was governed in the kingdom and opened the door for the Bible to circulate in English with greater freedom in the years that followed. For many ordinary people, this meant being able to hear the Word of God in the language they spoke at home, without depending on another language they did not understand.

But the story also leaves us a delicate lesson. Kings and authorities come and go, their decisions are debated, and over time they are judged in different ways. What the Scriptures teach clearly is that the true head of the Church is no human ruler, but Jesus Christ himself. The apostle Paul affirms this when he writes that Christ is the head of the body, the church.

At the end of the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus said that whoever hears his words and does them is like a wise man who built his house on the rock. The rain fell, the floods came, the winds blew, and the house did not fall, because it was founded on the rock. The events of nations change, but the Word of Christ remains firm for those who decide to build on it. So as we remember this date, we give thanks for the Word we can read today, and we look to Christ as the Lord to whom his Church belongs.

✝️ Today’s Message

It is easy to trust in what we can see: a position, an institution, a strong person, or a well-made plan. But history shows us that all of that can change from one year to the next. When we remember events like the Act of Supremacy of 1534, we see how human events move history, and we also understand that we need a foundation that does not depend on them.

Jesus expressed it with a simple image: two houses, two foundations. The difference was not that one withstood the storm and the other was spared it. Both received the same rain, the same wind, and the same floods. The difference was in the foundation. The one who heard and obeyed built on the rock. The one who heard and did not obey built on the sand.

Today you can do something concrete. Think of an area of your life where you have been trusting more in your own strength or in people than in God: your finances, your health, your family, or your work. Bring that area to God in prayer and ask the Lord which of his words you should obey in it. Perhaps it is forgiving someone, speaking the truth, being patient, or taking a first step you have put off. Obeying is laying a brick on the rock.

And when fear comes knocking at your door, remember the words of the psalmist: “God is our refuge and strength, a very present help in trouble. Therefore we will not fear.” Christ is the head of his Church and the Lord of your life. As long as you are united to him, no storm will have the last word. Build carefully today, with humility and obedience, and you will see that your house stands firm.

Go out into this day with the assurance that Christ is the rock that does not move. May his peace be your shelter, his Word your guide, and his presence your strength. Walk with confidence: the one who holds you is greater than any storm.
