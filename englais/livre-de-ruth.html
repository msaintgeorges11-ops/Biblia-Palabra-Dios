📖 Book of Ruth

Loyalty and redemption · 4 chapters

Background

The author of Ruth is not mentioned; Jewish tradition suggests Samuel. The story takes place in the time of the judges, when Israel was living in instability, and it was probably written in the time of the monarchy, since it ends with the genealogy of David. It tells the story of a family from Bethlehem that emigrated to Moab because of famine, and of the Moabite woman who returned with her mother-in-law.

Central message

Ruth shows that God works quietly, in everyday decisions, to care for those who trust him. A foreign widow finds refuge under the wings of the God of Israel, and a kind relative redeems her. Her story leads to the line of David and of Jesus. It is a tale of loyalty, of grace toward the poor and the foreigner, and of divine provision in the midst of pain.

Structure of the book

  • Chapter 1: the famine, the death of Elimelech and his sons in Moab, and the return of Naomi with Ruth to Bethlehem.
  • Chapter 2: Ruth gleans in Boaz's field, and he protects her and treats her kindly.
  • Chapter 3: the counsel of Naomi and Ruth on the threshing floor, asking Boaz to fulfill his role as kinsman-redeemer.
  • Chapter 4: Boaz redeems the inheritance before the elders, marries Ruth, and Obed is born.
  • Chapter 4: the final genealogy leading from Obed to Jesse and to David, showing God's design.

Main themes

The first theme is loyalty. Ruth could have returned to her own people, but she decided to stay with Naomi and to adopt her God. Her commitment was sacrificial, expecting nothing in return. This faithfulness reflects the Lord's covenant of love with his people and teaches us that human relationships endure through constant self-giving, more than through passing feelings.

The second theme is redemption. Boaz acts as kinsman-redeemer: he redeems the inheritance and takes the widow as his wife, restoring her name and hope. This figure points to Christ, who redeemed us by becoming our brother in the flesh. Boaz uses his position to care for a poor foreigner, which God himself commands and which honors his character.

The third theme is hidden providence. Throughout the book, there are no spectacular miracles, but God directs events: Ruth happens to arrive in Boaz's field, and from their marriage is born the grandfather of David. Naomi goes from bitterness to joy. The Lord weaves his plan even in times of famine and loss.

Key verses

  • Ruth 1:16
  • Ruth 2:12
  • Ruth 3:11
  • Ruth 4:14

Reflecting on the verses

Ruth 1:16 records Ruth's promise that Naomi's people would be her people and Naomi's God her God. Ruth 2:12 asks that the Lord reward the one who has taken refuge under his wings. In Ruth 3:11, Boaz acknowledges that the whole town knows that Ruth is a virtuous woman. And Ruth 4:14 blesses the Lord, who has not left Naomi without a redeemer.

Application for today

Ruth invites us to be loyal in small things: to accompany those who suffer, to care for family, and to serve without seeking recognition. It also encourages us when life seems empty, because God keeps working behind the everyday. And it reminds us that God's people must open their doors to the foreigner and the needy, as Boaz opened his field. When everything seems lost, let us remember that the Lord knows how to write endings of hope, even out of our moments of greatest loss, hunger, and distress. He never abandons the one who takes refuge in him.

Questions for reflection

  • Whom might God be calling me to accompany with loyalty in their pain?
  • How does it comfort me to know that Christ is my redeemer?
  • How can I show Boaz's generosity toward the foreigner or the poor?

Prayer

God of providence, thank you for caring for those who take refuge in you. Make me a faithful, generous person, attentive to those who suffer, and thank you for Jesus, my redeemer and your greatest provision. Amen.
