📖 Book of 3 John

Working Together with the Truth · 1 chapter

Context

The third letter of John is addressed to Gaius, a beloved and generous believer who welcomed into his home the traveling brothers who carried the Gospel from town to town. While 2 John warns against welcoming false teachers, 3 John praises hospitality toward true missionaries. In the same church, there was a leader named Diotrephes who opposed John's authority, rejected the brothers who were sent and expelled those who welcomed them. John writes to encourage Gaius and to correct this bad example.

Central Message

The central message is the importance of supporting those who serve the Lord and of walking in the truth with humility. John commends Gaius for his faithfulness and love, and reminds him that by supporting the workers, he becomes a fellow worker for the truth. At the same time, he warns against the danger of personal ambition, illustrated by Diotrephes, who loved to be first among them. The letter ends with the mention of Demetrius, a man who has received a good testimony from everyone.

Structure of the Book

  • Verses 1 to 4: greeting to Gaius, the wish that he prosper and John's joy at hearing that he walks in the truth.
  • Verses 5 to 8: the praise of hospitality toward the brothers and the duty to support them.
  • Verses 9 to 11: the bad example of Diotrephes and the exhortation to imitate what is good.
  • Verses 12 to 14: the good testimony of Demetrius and the final greetings.

Main Themes

The first theme is the joy of seeing the faith of others grow. John affirms that he has no greater joy than to hear that his children are walking in the truth. This is the desire of every teacher, parent or leader: not his own fame, but the spiritual good of those he accompanies. He also wishes that Gaius be in good health and prosper, just as his soul prospers, that is, that his inner life be strong.

The second theme is hospitality as a form of collaboration. Gaius was not doing missionary work directly, but by welcoming and supporting the brothers, he made the advance of the Gospel possible. John encourages him to send them on their journey in a manner worthy of God, for they set out for the sake of the Name, accepting nothing from unbelievers. Those who support through prayer, resources and affection are fellow workers for the truth.

The third theme is pride that divides. Diotrephes loved to be first, spoke wicked words against John and forbade others to welcome the brothers. His example warns against the danger of leadership centered on oneself. John urges us not to imitate evil but good, and reminds us that whoever does good is from God, while whoever does evil has not seen God.

Key Verses

  • 3 John 2
  • 3 John 4
  • 3 John 8
  • 3 John 11

Reflecting on the Verses

In 3 John 2, the apostle expresses his wish that Gaius be in good health and prosper, as his soul prospers. In 3 John 4, he affirms that he has no greater joy than to see his children walking in the truth. In 3 John 8, he encourages us to support the workers so as to be fellow workers for the truth. And in 3 John 11, he urges us not to imitate evil, but good, for whoever does good is from God.

Application for Today

This letter invites us to ask how we can support those who serve the Lord, whether missionaries, pastors, teachers or volunteers. An encouraging message, a meal, an offering or a prayer can make a great difference. It also challenges us to examine our heart: am I seeking to serve or to be recognized? Humility is the best defense against the ambition that divides communities.

Questions to Reflect On

  • Whom can I support through my prayer, my time or my resources?
  • Is there a desire for recognition that I need to hand over to the Lord?
  • What good example can I imitate this week?

Prayer

Lord, thank you for those who serve faithfully. Make me a fellow worker for the truth, free me from pride and teach me to imitate what is good and to practice hospitality. Amen.
