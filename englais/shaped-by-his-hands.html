Theme: Shaped by His Hands

Adelaide Pollard and Her Hymn

⏱ Approximate duration: 5 min

📖 Today’s Verses

Romans 9:20-21
Romans 8:28

🙏 Today’s Prayer

Lord and Potter of my life, I come before you this morning with open hands. Thank you for giving me one more day, and thank you for not leaving me half-made. You began a work in me, and I know you are continuing it, even when I sometimes resist and harden like a dried-out vessel.

Today I want to tell you sincerely: make of me what you will. I have my plans, my desires and my projects, but I recognize that you see what I cannot see. If something doesn’t go the way I hoped, help me believe that you are not absent and that all things work together for good for those who love you. Take away my pride, my impatience and my habit of wanting to control everything. Shape my character with love.

Give me a teachable heart to accept your direction. May every encounter today be a chance to reflect your goodness, and every setback a lesson in your hands. Take care of my family, my work and my decisions. When I feel tired, remind me that clay does not grow weary in the potter’s hands. And when I have to wait, teach me to rest in the fact that your timing is perfect. Thank you, because you are not finished with me. In Jesus’ name, amen.

📚 Today’s Story

On November 27, 1862, Adelaide Addison Pollard was born in Bloomfield, Iowa. She would become a Bible teacher and the author of more than a hundred hymns and gospel songs. She studied in several places, taught in girls’ schools, and later devoted herself to teaching the Scriptures from town to town.

From her youth she felt a desire to serve as a missionary in Africa. Around 1902 she worked to gather the necessary resources, but the money never came and the door did not open. It was a great disappointment. With a heavy heart, she attended a prayer meeting. There, an elderly woman asked the Lord to have his way in the lives of everyone present.

That simple prayer stayed in Adelaide’s heart. When she got home, she meditated on the passage in Jeremiah about the potter working the clay on his wheel. The vessel was spoiled in his hands, and he made it again into another vessel, as it seemed good to him. From that meditation sprang the words of a hymn we still know today: Have Thine Own Way, Lord, sung as a prayer of surrender to the Lord.

Years later, Adelaide taught at a missionary training school in Nyack, New York, and was able to go to Africa for a brief period before World War I. She died on December 20, 1934. Her life shows that a closed door does not mean a lost purpose. Sometimes God uses our disappointment to give the world a song of surrender.

✝️ Today’s Message

There are days when our plans run into a closed door. Adelaide Pollard experienced it with her desire to go to Africa, and her story reminds us that disappointment is not the end. When she surrendered her disillusionment to God, a hymn was born that has accompanied believers for many generations.

Paul uses the image of the potter in his letter to the Romans. He asks: will the thing formed say to the one who formed it, “Why have you made me like this?” The potter has rights over the clay. This is not a statement meant to frighten us, but to reassure us. The One who made us knows the design, knows how long the work takes, and does not make mistakes in the process.

In the same letter there is a promise that sustains those who wait: for those who love God, all things work together for their good. It does not say that all things are good, but that God directs them toward good. That includes disappointed plans, long waits, and letdowns we do not understand. Nothing remains outside his watchful hand.

For today, I suggest a concrete prayer of surrender. Think of something you have insisted on in your own way, and tell God about it frankly: Lord, let your will be done here. Then, as you go about your tasks, act with gentleness and attention to what God will place before you. Perhaps a conversation, a humble task, or a change of route. Whoever lets himself be shaped discovers that God did have a purpose.

Walk through this day with the peace of one who knows he is in good hands. What the Lord has begun in you, he will carry on to completion. Be courageous, be patient, and receive his grace with every step you take today.
